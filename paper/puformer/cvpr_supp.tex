\section{The exact adjoint of reflect padding}
\label{sec:adjoint}
Every gradient step on $\|\D\X-\YH\|^2$ needs $\D^{\top}=\mathcal{P}^{\top}\mathcal{K}^{\top}\mathcal{S}^{\top}$, where $\mathcal{P}$ pads by $p$ pixels, $\mathcal{K}$ is a valid correlation with the $(2p+1)\times(2p+1)$ kernel and $\mathcal{S}$ keeps every $s$-th pixel from index zero. $\mathcal{S}^{\top}$ inserts zeros and $\mathcal{K}^{\top}$ is a full convolution; together they are a strided transposed convolution with output padding $s-1$. The adjoint of reflect padding adds each padded margin back onto the pixels it was copied from. For a padded row index $j\in[0,H+2p)$ the source row is $p-j$ for $j<p$, $j-p$ in the interior and $2H-2+p-j$ in the bottom margin, hence
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
(\mathcal{P}^{\top}\mathbf{g})_i={}&\mathbf{g}_{i+p}+[1\le i\le p]\,\mathbf{g}_{p-i}\\
&+[H-1-p\le i\le H-2]\,\mathbf{g}_{2H-2+p-i},
\end{split}
\label{eq:fold}
\end{equation}
applied to rows and then to columns. With~\eqref{eq:fold}, the operator in the network is exactly the simulation operator, and $\langle\D\X,\mathbf{Y}\rangle=\langle\X,\D^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\rangle$ holds to a relative error below $10^{-13}$ in double precision at every scale factor. $\R^{\top}$ is the transpose of the response matrix.

\section{Proofs}
\label{sec:proofs}
\paragraph{Theorem~\mref{thm:kron}.} (a) By~(\mref{eq:modes}), $\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta$ applies $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}$ and $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}$ to the two spatial modes and the identity to the spectral mode, and $\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi$ applies $\mathbf{r}_\phi^{\top}\mathbf{r}_\phi$ to the spectral mode alone. Each factor is diagonalized by the corresponding factor of $\mathcal{Q}$, and an identity factor by any orthonormal basis. (b) In this basis the matrix of~(\mref{eq:normal}) is diagonal with entries $1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b>0$. (c) $\mathcal{N}(\D_\theta)\cap\mathcal{N}(\R_\phi)=\mathcal{N}(\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta+\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi)$, whose eigenvalues $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\nu_b$ are sums of nonnegative terms and vanish only when both terms do. The basis tensors with $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j>0$ span the row space of $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\otimes\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}$, of dimension $hw$, and those with $\nu_b>0$ the row space of $\mathbf{r}_\phi$, of dimension $M$. The orthogonal projector onto the null space is therefore $(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{s}})\otimes(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{r}}\otimes\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{c}})$ in mode order, with $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{s}},\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{r}},\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{c}}$ the projectors onto the row spaces of $\mathbf{r}_\phi,\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}},\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}$, and its dimension is $(B-M)(HW-hw)$. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Theorem~\mref{thm:mismatch}.} (a) $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\X^*-\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{0}$ makes both updates vanish. (b) With $\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, a direct computation gives $T(\X^*)-\X^*=(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{e}$ with $\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$. In the singular value decomposition of $\mathbf{B}$, $(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\top}$ has singular values $b_i/(1+b_i^2)\le\tfrac12$. Similarly $G(\X^*)-\X^*=\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{e}$, whose norm is at most $\|\mathbf{B}\|\,\|\mathbf{e}\|$. (c) $T_k$ is nonexpansive, since $\|T_k(\mathbf{V})-T_k(\mathbf{V}')\|=\|(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}(\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{V}')\|\le\|\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{V}'\|$. With $e_k=\|\X_k-\X^*\|$ and $(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}_k)(\X^*)=\X^*$,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
e_k&\le L_k\|T_k(\X_{k-1})-\X^*\|\\
&\le L_k\big(\|T_k(\X_{k-1})-T_k(\X^*)\|+\|T_k(\X^*)-\X^*\|\big)\le L_k\big(e_{k-1}+\tfrac12\|\mathbf{e}_k\|\big),
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
and unrolling from $e_0=0$ gives the bound. The gradient step is not nonexpansive in general, so the bound has no counterpart for it. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Corollary~\mref{cor:border}.} A low-resolution pixel is a weighted sum of the $(2p+1)\times(2p+1)$ high-resolution pixels around its sampling position. Two border rules give the same value whenever this window lies inside the image, i.e., unless the sampling position is within $p$ pixels of the border; with sampling positions $0,s,2s,\dots$ this concerns the first $\lceil p/s\rceil$ low-resolution rows and columns (and, at the far border, those whose window crosses it). \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Theorem~\mref{thm:ident}.} (a) On vectorized images, $\D_\theta=\mathbf{I}_B\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta$ and $\R_\phi=\mathbf{r}_\phi\otimes\mathbf{I}_N$, with $\bar{\D}_\theta$ the single-band spatial operator and $N=HW$. By the mixed-product rule, $(\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta)(\mathbf{r}_\phi\otimes\mathbf{I}_N)=\mathbf{r}_\phi\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta=(\mathbf{r}_\phi\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N/s^2})(\mathbf{I}_B\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta)$. With $\YM=\R_{\phi^*}\X$ and $\YH=\D_{\theta^*}\X$, $\D_{\theta^*}\YM=\R_{\phi^*}\D_{\theta^*}\X=\R_{\phi^*}\YH$.
(b) By (a), $\mathbf{r}(\theta,\phi)=\D_\theta\R_{\phi^*}\X-\R_\phi\D_{\theta^*}\X=\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}\X$ with $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}=\mathbf{r}_{\phi^*}\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta-\mathbf{r}_\phi\otimes\bar{\D}_{\theta^*}$, so $J=0$ exactly on its null space. Suppose $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}=\mathbf{0}$ and apply it to $\X=\mathbf{e}_b\otimes\mathbf{u}$, a single band $b$ with spatial pattern $\mathbf{u}$: $(\mathbf{r}_{\phi^*})_{:b}\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta\mathbf{u}=(\mathbf{r}_\phi)_{:b}\otimes\bar{\D}_{\theta^*}\mathbf{u}$ for all $\mathbf{u}$. If $\bar{\D}_\theta\neq\bar{\D}_{\theta^*}$, then $\bar{\D}_\theta\neq c\,\bar{\D}_{\theta^*}$ for every scalar $c$: both kernels sum to one and reflect padding preserves constants, so both operators map a constant image to the same constant, which forces $c=1$. Two linear maps of rank greater than one that are not scalar multiples of each other map some $\mathbf{u}$ to linearly independent images, and for this $\mathbf{u}$ the identity forces $(\mathbf{r}_{\phi^*})_{:b}=(\mathbf{r}_\phi)_{:b}=\mathbf{0}$, impossible for a response that sums to one. If $\bar{\D}_\theta=\bar{\D}_{\theta^*}$, the identity gives $(\mathbf{r}_{\phi^*})_{:b}=(\mathbf{r}_\phi)_{:b}$ for every $b$. Hence $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}\neq\mathbf{0}$ unless both operators coincide, and its null space is a proper subspace. Examples: a spatially constant image $\X=\mathbf{s}\otimes\mathbf{1}$ gives $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi^*}\X=\mathbf{r}_{\phi^*}\mathbf{s}\otimes(\bar{\D}_\theta\mathbf{1}-\bar{\D}_{\theta^*}\mathbf{1})=\mathbf{0}$ for every $\theta$; an image with equal bands $\X=\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{u}$ gives $\mathbf{r}_\phi\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}$ for every $\phi$, so $\mathcal{C}_{\theta^*\phi}\X=\mathbf{0}$. Over a finite set of injectively parametrized candidates the union of these null spaces still has measure zero, which recovers uniqueness on a grid for almost every $\X$.
(c) Write $\vartheta=(\theta,\phi)$ and $F(\vartheta,\mathbf{N})=\mathbf{G}(\vartheta,\mathbf{N})^{\top}\mathbf{r}(\vartheta,\mathbf{N})$, the gradient of $J/2$, where the noise enters as $\mathbf{r}(\vartheta,\mathbf{N})=\mathbf{r}(\vartheta,\mathbf{0})+\D_\theta\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}}-\R_\phi\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}}$. At $(\vartheta^*,\mathbf{0})$, $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{0}$, so $\partial F/\partial\vartheta=\mathbf{G}^{\top}\mathbf{G}+\sum_i r_i\nabla^2r_i=\mathbf{G}^{\top}\mathbf{G}$, which is invertible by assumption. The implicit function theorem gives a unique smooth stationary point $\hat\vartheta(\mathbf{N})$ near $\vartheta^*$ with $\hat\vartheta-\vartheta^*=-(\mathbf{G}^{\top}\mathbf{G})^{-1}\mathbf{G}^{\top}\mathbf{n}+O(\|\mathbf{N}\|^2)=-\mathbf{G}^{+}\mathbf{n}+O(\|\mathbf{N}\|^2)$, and $\|\mathbf{G}^{+}\mathbf{n}\|\le\|\mathbf{n}\|/\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G})$; under white noise its covariance is $\mathbf{G}^{+}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{G}^{+\top}$. The zero is isolated because $\mathbf{r}(\vartheta,\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{G}(\vartheta-\vartheta^*)+O(\|\vartheta-\vartheta^*\|^2)$ gives $\|\mathbf{r}\|\ge\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G})\|\vartheta-\vartheta^*\|-O(\|\vartheta-\vartheta^*\|^2)>0$ for small $\vartheta\neq\vartheta^*$. The second-order term contains the bias of the next paragraph. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Corollary~\mref{cor:chain}.} With $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{A}_{\hat\vartheta}$, $(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*=-\mathbf{A}'(\hat\vartheta-\vartheta^*)+O(\|\hat\vartheta-\vartheta^*\|^2)$, so $\|\mathbf{e}\|\le\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\mathbf{A}'\|\,\|\hat\vartheta-\vartheta^*\|$ to first order; Theorem~\mref{thm:mismatch}(b) and part (c) give the bound. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Noise in the criterion $J$.} With independent noise, $\mathbb{E}\,J(\theta,\phi)=\|\mathbf{E}_{\theta\phi}\X\|^2+\tfrac{N}{s^2}\big(M\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2\|\mathbf{k}_\theta\|^2+\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2\|\mathbf{r}_\phi\|_F^2\big)$ up to border terms, where $\mathbf{k}_\theta$ is the blur kernel. The noise term decreases with $\theta$ (a wider kernel has a smaller norm) and barely depends on $\phi$, so noise biases $\hat\theta$ upward by an amount that vanishes as the SNR grows.

\paragraph{Proposition~\mref{prop:kl}.} Let $\varsigma_{bij}=\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b$, the eigenvalues of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, and $e^{f}$ the numerator of $\Gamma$, so $\tfrac12<e^{f}<2$. (a) $\mathbf{G}_k=\mathbf{0}$ at $\X^*$. (b) The step maps a difference $\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{V}'$ to $\mathcal{Q}\big((1-\Gamma\varsigma)\odot\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{V}')\big)$, and $0\le\Gamma\varsigma=e^{f}\varsigma/(1+\varsigma)<2$, so every factor lies in $(-1,1]$. (c) The displacement is $\mathcal{Q}\big(\Gamma\odot\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{e})\big)$, whose operator norm is $\max e^{f}\sqrt{\varsigma}/(1+\varsigma)\le2\cdot\tfrac12=1$. \hfill$\square$

\section{Data and protocol}
\label{sec:data}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Chikusei.} The scene~\cite{yokoya2016chikusei} has $2517\times2335$ pixels and 128 bands from 363 to 1018~nm. The split sizes follow~\cite{chen2026twostage}: eight $256\times256$ test images, 64 validation patches and 832 training patches of $64\times64$. Their locations are not specified there, so we fix them: we take the central $2048\times2048$ crop, which has no no-data border, and divide it by its global maximum (15{,}133 digital numbers, DN). The test images are the eight $256\times256$ tiles of the top 256 rows, the validation patches tile rows 272--400, and training draws random $64\times64$ crops with dihedral augmentation from rows 416--2048. Sixteen-row gaps keep the regions disjoint.

\paragraph{Pavia Centre.} The ROSIS scene~\cite{gamba2004pavia} has $1096\times715$ pixels and 102 bands between 430 and 860~nm. As in~\cite{chen2026twostage} we keep bands 11--102 (92 bands) and use a four-band multispectral image. The benchmark files distributed with PSRT~\cite{deng2023psrt} were not accessible, so we rebuild the benchmark from the original scene to the same specification: division by the maximum of the retained bands (8{,}000~DN), four $256\times256$ test tiles in the top-left $512\times512$ region, 63 validation patches on a 32-pixel grid in rows 528--656, and random training crops from rows 672--1096 and from the strip to the right of the test region (rows 0--512, columns 528--715), with 16-pixel gaps.

\paragraph{CAVE and Harvard.} CAVE~\cite{yasuma2010cave} has 32 indoor scenes of $512\times512$ pixels and 31 bands (400--700~nm); the first 20 scenes train and the last 12 test, as in MHF-Net, MoG-DCN and DCT. Harvard~\cite{chakrabarti2011harvard} has $1040\times1392$ images with 31 bands (420--720~nm); 30 images train and the central $512\times512$ crops of 20 images test, each image divided by its own maximum. Validation patches come from the bottom-right corners of the training images. Both are paired with a Nikon RGB response.

\paragraph{NEON (real data).} The NEON airborne observation platform~\cite{kampe2010neon} records an imaging spectrometer (1~m) and an RGB camera (0.1~m) on the same flights. We use eight tiles of the SERC site (2019), 120 spectrometer bands between 400 and 1000~nm, and the camera image resampled to 0.25~m. The camera's spectral response is not published, so we fit a unit-sum nonnegative response of the three camera channels to the spectrometer bands on co-located 4~m blocks ($R^2$ between 0.45 and 0.79). At reduced resolution, the measured 1~m spectrometer image is the reference, the LR-HSI is simulated from it at $\times4$, and the measured camera image is the HR-MSI; at full resolution the measured 1~m image is fused with the 0.25~m camera image and evaluated without a reference ($D_\lambda$, $D_s$, HQNR~\cite{alparone2008qnr}). \Cref{tab:neon} lists the results.

\qoneif{sjer-puformer-psnr}{\paragraph{A second site.} To test across scenes, the networks trained on SERC (Maryland) are tested without retraining on SJER (California, 2019), a savanna site with other vegetation, soil and illumination. The camera response is fitted on three SJER tiles ($R^2$ 0.69--0.78), and \qone{sjer-n} crops of five other tiles form the test set; on two of them the fitted response explains the camera poorly ($R^2\le0.6$), and we keep them. ExactFuse reaches \qone{sjer-puformer-psnr}~dB, \qone{sjer-gain}~dB above the best of the \qone{sjer-n-baselines} retrained networks tested there (\qone{sjer-best}, \qone{sjer-best-psnr}~dB), and has the lowest SAM (\cref{tab:sjer}).}

\paragraph{Spectral responses.} WorldView-2 (Chikusei) and IKONOS (Pavia Centre) publish their band responses only as plots with band edges. We model each band as a flat top with logistic flanks (4~nm roll-off) that pass through the published edges, at 5\% response for WorldView-2 and at 50\% for the IKONOS band limits (blue 445--516, green 506--595, red 632--698 and near infrared 757--853~nm), and normalize every band to unit sum.

\paragraph{Degradation.} Every image, including every training crop, is degraded on its own with~(\mref{eq:obs}): reflect padding, a normalized Gaussian blur ($7\times7$, $\sigma=2$ at $\times4$; $(2s-1)\times(2s-1)$, $\sigma=s/2$ at $\times s$), decimation from index zero, and the spectral response. At $\times16$ all methods train on $128\times128$ patches with batch 4.

\section{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}
All published networks are built from their authors' repositories, cloned at run time; only the numbers of input and output bands and the scale factor are changed. PSRT: github.com/Deng-shangqi/PSRT (9c2be90); DCT: github.com/qingma2016/DCTransformer (bd75b15); MIMFormer: github.com/meiruni/MIMFormer (0904158); DHIF-Net: github.com/TaoHuang95/DHIF-Net (6434366), whose code covers $\times8$ and $\times16$, so at $\times4$ we use one of the two stride-4 layers of its $\times16$ operator; SMGU-Net: github.com/yansql/SMGU-Net (115ce77), written for $\times4$ only; CLSNet: github.com/HengYang01/CLSNet (6b13371), whose upsampling layer is set to the scale factor; OTPNet: github.com/tsharry2201/OTPNet (7c17964), written for pansharpening, with the HR-MSI in place of the panchromatic image; U2Net: github.com/PSRben/U2Net (32e214c), also written for pansharpening, with its input, upsampling and output layers resized; its attention spans all pixels, so a $256\times256$ test image does not fit a 16~GB T4, and it is tested in overlapping $128\times128$ tiles (32-pixel overlap, averaged); MIMO-SST: github.com/Freelancefangjian/MIMO-SST (06aacc1), whose final full-resolution output is used. DCT and MIMFormer use gradient checkpointing to fit a 16~GB T4, which does not change what they compute. SSR-NET is our re-implementation of~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet}.

Under the common recipe every baseline uses its published learning rate and an L1 loss with AdamW ($\beta=(0.9,0.99)$, no weight decay, i.e., Adam), batch 8, a 1000-iteration warm-up and cosine decay over the budget, and no gradient clipping; ExactFuse uses the same pipeline with its own learning rate, weight decay and clipping. The authors' own recipes (\cref{tab:margin}) are read from their code: PSRT uses AdamW (learning rate $10^{-4}$, decay $10^{-4}$), clipping at 10, batch 32 and a constant learning rate; MIMFormer uses Adam ($10^{-4}$), batch 16 and step decay at 30, 60 and 90 of 101 epochs, mapped onto the time budget.

The image path, including all operator applications, runs in 32-bit precision and the priors in 16-bit mixed precision. The weights with the best validation PSNR are tested once, without self-ensemble.

\paragraph{Blur of any shape.} The identifiability residual $\D_k\YM-\R_\phi\YH$ is linear in the kernel $k$, so a kernel of any shape is the least-squares solution over all bands and low-resolution pixels, with taps that sum to one (\qone{pc-np-s}~ms per image). On noise-free pairs its relative error against the true kernel is \qone{np-chikusei-ng-kerr}\% on Chikusei and \qone{np-pavia-ng-kerr}\% on Pavia Centre over the out-of-family blurs, and the network given it reaches the PSNR it reaches with the true kernel (any-shape row of Tab.~\mref{tab:cond}). Its price is noise sensitivity: at 30~dB SNR the kernel error grows to \qone{np-chikusei-ng-kerr30}\% and \qone{np-pavia-ng-kerr30}\%, and the PSNR is \qone{np-chikusei-ng-snr30-cost} and \qone{np-pavia-ng-snr30-cost}~dB below that with the isotropic estimate; at the training operator the two agree within 0.05~dB. \paragraph{Stability of the operator estimate.} \Cref{tab:opan} checks Theorem~\mref{thm:ident}(c) on the test images. The Jacobian has full rank for all five parameters, with condition numbers of \qone{opan-chikusei-condnorm} (Chikusei) and \qone{opan-pavia-condnorm} (Pavia Centre) after scaling its columns, so the parameters are nearly uncorrelated. For the grid offsets and the response shift the first-order prediction matches the measured error; for the blur widths the measured error exceeds it as the SNR falls, because noise also enters the Jacobian and biases the blur estimate upward (next paragraph), an effect of second order that the first-order bound does not contain. Even at 20~dB SNR the errors stay far below the grid step of 0.05~px. The conditioning is as good for two different blur widths (\qone{opan-chikusei-condnorm-aniso}) and with grid offsets and a response shift (\qone{opan-chikusei-condnorm-off}). For scenes in the null space of $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}$ the corresponding columns of $\mathbf{G}$ vanish, as part (b) states: for spatially constant scenes the blur and offset columns fall to \qone{opan-chikusei-deg-const-col0} and \qone{opan-chikusei-deg-const-col2} (Chikusei), and for scenes with equal bands the response-shift column falls to \qone{opan-chikusei-deg-flat-col4}. The first-order relative operator residual used in the corollary matches the true one closely for blur errors up to 10\% (\qone{opan-chikusei-res10-lin}\% against \qone{opan-chikusei-res10}\% at $+10\%$ on Chikusei).

\paragraph{Which assumptions hold for the trained network.} Theorem~\mref{thm:mismatch}(a)--(b) and Proposition~\mref{prop:kl} hold for every value of the learned weights: the step sizes are positive by construction (softplus) and the learned gains lie in $(\tfrac12,2)$ by construction, so the trained data steps keep the ground truth fixed and are nonexpansive. Part (c) needs priors with $\mathcal{P}_k(\X^*)=\mathbf{0}$ and a Lipschitz constant $L_k$, which training does not enforce; we therefore do not claim convergence of the unrolled network, and report instead that \qone{null-chikusei-puformer-share}\% of ExactFuse's error lies in the fusion null space (Tab.~\mref{tab:bench}), i.e., its output keeps the observed part.

\paragraph{Cost.} \Cref{tab:cost} measures the whole pipeline on a T4. The operator estimate takes \qone{pc-iso-s}~s (isotropic grid), \qone{pc-wide-s}~s (wide family) or \qone{pc-np-s}~ms (nonparametric kernel), the eigendecompositions \qone{pc-eig-s}~s once per image size, and the network \qone{pc-net-s}~s. The closed-form step costs about as much as a gradient step (\qone{pc-grad-s}~s), which reproduces the LR-HSI 6.7~dB worse (Tab.~\mref{tab:ablation}), and less than a conjugate-gradient step (\qone{pc-cg5-s} and \qone{pc-cg10-s}~s with 5 and 10 iterations). The time grows about linearly with the number of pixels (\qone{pc-size1024-s}~s at $1024\times1024$, against \qone{pc-size1024-mim-s}~s for MIMFormer) and slowly with the number of bands.

\paragraph{One network for any sensor and scale factor.} Every input of the prior lies in the hyperspectral bands, so one ExactFuse network can be trained on random multispectral responses, a new one for every batch, and applied to any sensor (\cref{tab:family}). Tested on seven real sensors, none of them seen in training, it exceeds GSA by \qone{fam-chikusei-gsa-gain-min}--\qone{fam-chikusei-gsa-gain-max}~dB on Chikusei and \qone{fam-pavia-gsa-gain-min}--\qone{fam-pavia-gsa-gain-max}~dB on Pavia Centre. \section{Open problems and future directions}
\label{sec:future}
Most fusion methods, ours included in its benchmark setting, assume co-registered inputs, white noise of one level, a known spatially invariant blur and simultaneous acquisitions. We list the gaps that remain and how the exact-operator view gives each a concrete entry point.

\paragraph{Registered and unregistered pixels.} Real pairs are misregistered locally (terrain and building parallax, moving objects, different view angles), so one image contains registered and unregistered pixels. A global sub-pixel offset fits into the separable operator and is estimated from the pair with the criterion of Theorem~\mref{thm:ident}; a local displacement does not. The confidence-weighted data step treats unregistered pixels as unreliable measurements and its map $1-w$ marks them, which turns a nuisance into an output; a spatially varying operator (a displacement field estimated jointly with the image) is the principled next step.

\paragraph{Realistic noise.} Hyperspectral noise depends on the band (low SNR at the band edges and in absorption bands) and on the signal (photon noise), and pushbroom sensors add stripes. Per-band noise levels enter the MAP derivation as per-band weights of the data term; with the imputation form of the confidence step they keep the closed form. Training and evaluation with band- and signal-dependent noise, and with stripes, should replace the white-noise protocol.

\paragraph{Spatially varying and unknown blur.} Real point-spread functions vary over the field of view and are rarely Gaussian. Non-separable kernels keep the gradient and conjugate-gradient steps but lose the closed form; estimating a nonparametric kernel from $\D\YM=\R\YH$ (Theorem~\mref{thm:ident}) and learning operator families from calibration data are open.

\paragraph{Changes between acquisition dates.} Hyperspectral and multispectral images of the same scene are often taken days apart (e.g., EnMAP or PRISMA with Sentinel-2). Classical robust fusion models the change as a sparse outlier~\cite{ferraris2017robust}; the confidence step is a learned counterpart whose map is a change detector, and joint fusion and change detection on real multi-date pairs is a natural application.

\paragraph{Real references and trustworthy outputs.} Wald's protocol simulates both inputs, so it cannot measure robustness to the real operator. Real paired benchmarks (e.g., airborne spectrometer and camera flights, or lens-based capture), no-reference metrics that respect the null space, and calibrated uncertainty for the part of the image that only a prior supplies are needed before fused products are used for quantitative analysis.

\paragraph{Other inverse problems with Kronecker structure.} The same operators describe hyperspectral pansharpening ($M=1$), spectral super-resolution from RGB (no $\D$), multispectral pansharpening and multi-temporal or multi-sensor fusion; the closed-form data step, the null-space split and the band-space interface carry over to each.
