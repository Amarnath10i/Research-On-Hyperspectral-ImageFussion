\title{ExactFuse: Closed-Form Unfolding with Exact Operators for Sensor-Independent Hyperspectral and Multispectral Image Fusion}

\author{\cvprauthors}   % hidden in review mode

\begin{abstract}
A hyperspectral camera records hundreds of bands but sees coarsely; a multispectral camera sees finely but records only a few bands. Deep unfolding networks fuse the two well, yet they learn or approximate the blur and spectral response, and each new sensor pair means retraining. ExactFuse instead builds on exact operators. Given a separable blur and the border rule of the simulation, the data step has a closed form, and the part of the image that neither observation constrains, 88\% of the unknowns on Chikusei, becomes explicit. For almost every scene the two observations pin down the true operators, so we estimate them at test time. With an exact data step the true image stays put; a wrong operator moves it by a bounded amount, whatever the weights. The prior sees the multispectral image only as spectra in the hyperspectral bands and is trained over a range of blurs, responses and noise, so one network serves several sensors. Against \qone{n-retrained-word} published networks retrained from their authors' code under a common budget, ExactFuse wins on Chikusei and Pavia Centre, by \qone{chikusei4-gain} and \qone{pavia4-gain}~dB, and in eight further settings up to $\times16$, and reproduces the low-resolution input \qone{chikusei4-cons-gain}~dB better than the strongest baseline. Most of the gain comes from the prior and how it is trained; the exact operators pay off when the blur, sensor or noise changes. Like every network trained on registered pairs, ExactFuse loses over 15~dB to a half-pixel misregistration; registering first recovers most of it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
A hyperspectral camera measures hundreds of narrow bands at coarse resolution; a multispectral camera measures a few broad bands at fine resolution. Fuse them and you get a high-resolution hyperspectral image (HR-HSI)~\cite{vivone2025deep}. Most recent methods are deep networks trained on simulated pairs: convolutional networks~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet}, transformers~\cite{li2024mimformer,deng2023psrt,ma2024reciprocal}, state-space models~\cite{pei2026s3o} and diffusion models~\cite{chen2026twostage}.

Deep unfolding networks~\cite{xie2022mhfnet,dong2021mogdcn,huang2022dhif} unroll an iterative solver. At each stage a data step shrinks the mismatch with the observations, then a learned prior takes over. That data step needs the blur, the downsampling and the spectral response. Most unfolding networks learn them as convolutions or pad with zeros, even though the benchmark data come from a known blur and another border rule. The result is odd: the ground truth itself fails the network's consistency check. And every trained network is tied to one sensor pair.

Evaluation adds its own trouble. Papers differ in splits, responses, normalization and metric code~\cite{vivone2025deep}. PSRT~\cite{deng2023psrt} is reported at 44.60~dB PSNR on Chikusei $\times$4 in~\cite{pei2026s3o} and at 50.67~dB in~\cite{chen2026twostage}; copied numbers cannot rank methods.

Our question is what exact operators buy an unfolded fusion network. We pose fusion as maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation with parametric operators, unroll half-quadratic splitting (HQS)~\cite{zhang2020usrnet} and study the data step that results. We contribute:
\begin{itemize}
\item A theory of exact operators in unfolded fusion, linking observation noise to reconstruction error. With a separable blur and the right border rule, the data step is closed-form and the fusion null space explicit (88\% of the unknowns on Chikusei). The pair of observations identifies the operators over continuous parameters; we give an exact condition for when two sensor setups look the same and a stability bound under noise. An operator error shifts the reconstruction by an amount bounded independently of the learned weights (\cref{sec:theory}). Learned or zero-padded operators give none of this.
\item ExactFuse, an unfolded network with exact, swappable operators and a learned spectral data step that keeps these guarantees. Its inputs all live in the hyperspectral bands, so one set of weights fits any multispectral sensor of known response. We train it over a family of observation models and estimate the operators at test time (\cref{sec:method}). The building blocks are standard; we do not claim them.
\item An equal-budget benchmark of \qone{n-retrained-word} published networks, retrained from their authors' code, with the error split into an observed and a null-space part to show where each method fails (\cref{sec:exp}). It also traces ExactFuse's lead: mostly to the prior and its training, which stay ahead under a shared recipe. The exact operators keep the output consistent with the inputs and matter most when the operator changes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Deep fusion networks} map the two observations straight to the HR-HSI and ignore the observation model. They build on convolutions~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet}, transformers~\cite{li2024mimformer,deng2023psrt,ma2024reciprocal,fang2024mimosst,yang2026clsnet}, general fusion backbones~\cite{peng2023u2net}, state-space models~\cite{pei2026s3o} or diffusion~\cite{chen2026twostage}. SSA~\cite{liang2026ssa} slices its kernels by the number of bands and works at any scale, so one model covers several sensors; their spectral responses, though, are not modeled.

\paragraph{Unfolding and operator knowledge.} Unrolling turns the iterations of a solver into network stages. MHF-Net~\cite{xie2022mhfnet}, MoG-DCN~\cite{dong2021mogdcn} and DHIF-Net~\cite{huang2022dhif} unfold fusion solvers with learned or approximate operators; later work refines the prior or the proximal update~\cite{yu2025otpnet}. IR\&ArF~\cite{qu2025irarf} unfolds fusion of unregistered images and learns the registration. For single-image super-resolution, USRNet~\cite{zhang2020usrnet} feeds the blur kernel to a closed-form data step with periodic borders, and blind methods train on random degradations~\cite{zhang2021bsrgan}. We carry the idea over to fusion, where there are two operators, and keep the simulation's border rule.

\paragraph{Closed forms and separable operators.} FUSE~\cite{wei2015fuse} and the Hessian-inversion method of Pineau et al.~\cite{pineau2023exact} solve the quadratic fusion problem in closed form but assume periodic borders. Tensor methods~\cite{li2018cstf} use separable degradations to prove low-rank models recoverable. Our closed form holds for the reflect border of the simulation and runs as a differentiable layer whose operators can change per image. Its learned filters resemble deep Wiener deconvolution~\cite{dong2020wiener}, but are bounded so the step stays nonexpansive.

\paragraph{Range and null space.} Range--null-space networks~\cite{chen2020decomposition} split the output into a part fixed by the measurements and a null-space part; back-projection has made pansharpening more consistent~\cite{vicinanza2014consistency}. With exact separable operators this projection is closed-form (\cref{thm:kron}), and we use it to break down every method's error.

\section{Exact operators in unfolded fusion}
\label{sec:theory}
\subsection{Observation model and unfolding}
\label{sec:hqs}
Let $\X\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times H\times W}$ denote the HR-HSI. The two observations are modeled as
\begin{equation}
\YH=\D_\theta\X+\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}},\qquad \YM=\R_\phi\X+\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}},
\label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
where $\D_\theta$ applies reflect padding to each band, followed by a Gaussian blur of width $\theta$ and decimation by a factor $s$ starting at index zero, so that $\YH\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times h\times w}$ with $h=H/s$ and $w=W/s$. The operator $\R_\phi$ applies the $M$ spectral responses of the multispectral sensor to each pixel, shifted in wavelength by $\phi$~nm, and $\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}},\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}}$ are white Gaussian noise with standard deviations $\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}},\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}$. The benchmarks use $s=4$, a $7\times7$ blur with $\theta=\sigma_0=2$, $\phi=0$ and no noise~\cite{chen2026twostage}. We code $\D_\theta$ with its exact adjoint, where the transpose of reflect padding adds each padded margin back onto the pixels it was copied from (\supp). In double precision the adjoint test $\langle\D\X,\mathbf{Y}\rangle=\langle\X,\D^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\rangle$ holds to $10^{-13}$.

Assuming a prior $p(\X)\propto\exp(-\lambda\Phi(\X))$, the MAP estimate minimizes $\tfrac{1}{2\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2}\|\D_\theta\X-\YH\|^2+\tfrac{1}{2\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2}\|\R_\phi\X-\YM\|^2+\lambda\Phi(\X)$. HQS introduces an auxiliary image $\Z$, coupled to $\X$ through the penalty $\tfrac{\mu_k}{2}\|\Z-\X\|^2$, and alternates between a data step and a prior step, $\X_k=\mathrm{prox}_{(\lambda/\mu_k)\Phi}(\Z_k)$. Setting $\alpha_k=1/(\mu_k\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2)$ and $\beta_k=1/(\mu_k\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2)$, the data step reduces to the linear system
\begin{equation}
(\mathbf{I}+\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi)\Z_k=\X_{k-1}+\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}\YH+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}\YM,
\label{eq:normal}
\end{equation}
and a single gradient step from $\X_{k-1}$ gives its linearization,
\begin{equation}
\Z_k\approx\X_{k-1}-\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\D_\theta\X_{k-1}-\YH)-\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\X_{k-1}-\YM).
\label{eq:linstep}
\end{equation}
An unfolded network swaps the proximal map for a learned module. The derivation then tells us two things. The ratio $\beta_k/\alpha_k=\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2/\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2$ ought to track how noisy each observation is relative to the other. And the operators enter only through $\D_\theta$, $\R_\phi$ and their adjoints, so they can be swapped at test time with no retraining.

\subsection{Separable structure and the fusion null space}
\label{sec:kron}
An axis-aligned Gaussian blur is separable, and padding and decimation treat rows and columns independently. So the operators factor exactly, border included:
\begin{equation}
\D_\theta\X=\X\times_2\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\times_3\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}},\qquad \R_\phi\X=\X\times_1\mathbf{r}_\phi,
\label{eq:modes}
\end{equation}
where $\times_n$ denotes the mode-$n$ product, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times H}$ and $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{w\times W}$ perform padding, blurring and decimation along one axis, and $\mathbf{r}_\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times B}$ contains the spectral responses.
\begin{theorem}[Closed-form data step and null space]
\label{thm:kron}
Let $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{r}})\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\top}$, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{c}})\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top}$ and $\mathbf{r}_\phi^{\top}\mathbf{r}_\phi=\mathbf{V}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\nu})\mathbf{V}^{\top}$, and let $\mathcal{Q}(\cdot)=(\cdot)\times_1\mathbf{V}\times_2\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}\times_3\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}$.
(a) $\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta$ and $\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi$ are diagonal in the orthonormal basis $\mathcal{Q}$, with eigenvalues $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j$ and $\nu_b$.
(b) The solution of~\eqref{eq:normal} is
\begin{equation}
\Z_k=\mathcal{Q}\Big(\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{C}_k)\oslash\big[1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b\big]_{bij}\Big),
\label{eq:kronstep}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{C}_k$ the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:normal} and $\oslash$ element-wise division.
(c) The joint null space $\mathcal{N}(\D_\theta)\cap\mathcal{N}(\R_\phi)$ is spanned by the basis tensors with $\nu_b=0$ and $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j=0$. If $\mathbf{r}_\phi$, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}$ and $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}$ have full row rank, its dimension is $(B-M)(HW-hw)$, a fraction $(1-M/B)(1-1/s^2)$ of the unknowns.
\end{theorem}
The proof (\supp) rests on the mixed-product rule, with each factor of $\mathcal{Q}$ diagonalizing one mode. Step~\eqref{eq:kronstep} takes three mode products each way, $O(BHW(B+H+W))$ operations, and no iterations. It works for any separable kernel (Gaussian or not), any border rule and sub-pixel offset per axis, and any linear spectral response. All it needs are eigendecompositions of an $H\times H$, a $W\times W$ and a $B\times B$ matrix, cached per image size; FUSE~\cite{wei2015fuse}, by contrast, needs periodic borders. A non-separable or spatially varying kernel breaks the Kronecker structure, and the network falls back to a conjugate-gradient step with the same operator. Part (c) says what every method has to infer from a prior: the high spatial frequencies of the spectral directions the multispectral sensor misses. That is 88\% of the unknowns on Chikusei and 90\% on Pavia Centre at $\times4$ (with no HR-MSI, all high frequencies, $1-1/s^2$ of them). Spatial detail is mostly shared across bands, so the $M$ observed directions hold \qone{msi-chikusei4-detail}\% of its energy on Chikusei and \qone{msi-pavia4-detail}\% on Pavia Centre. The minimum-norm image consistent with both observations reaches \qone{msi-chikusei4-psnr-both}~dB on Chikusei; the LR-HSI alone gives \qone{msi-chikusei4-psnr-lr-only}~dB. \Cref{sec:nullsplit} splits errors along this null space.

\subsection{The cost of an inexact operator}
\label{sec:mismatch}
Stack the data terms as $\mathbf{A}=[\D_\theta;\R_\phi]$, $\mathbf{Y}=[\YH;\YM]$ and $\mathbf{W}_k=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_k\mathbf{I},\beta_k\mathbf{I})$, and let $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ be the operator a network really uses: zero-bordered, learned, or with wrong parameters. We write $T(\mathbf{V})=(\mathbf{I}+\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k\tilde{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}(\mathbf{V}+\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{Y})$ for the exact data step and $G(\mathbf{V})=\mathbf{V}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{Y})$ for its linearization.
\begin{theorem}[Fixed point and operator mismatch]
\label{thm:mismatch}
Let $\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{A}\X^*$. (a) If $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{A}$, then $T(\X^*)=G(\X^*)=\X^*$ for all $\alpha_k,\beta_k>0$. (b) For any $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, with $\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$,
\begin{equation}
\|T(\X^*)-\X^*\|\le\tfrac12\|\mathbf{e}\|,\qquad \|G(\X^*)-\X^*\|\le\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\|\,\|\mathbf{e}\|.
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
(c) If stage $k$ computes $\X_k=(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}_k)(T_k(\X_{k-1}))$ with a residual prior that satisfies $\mathcal{P}_k(\X^*)=\mathbf{0}$ and makes $\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}_k$ $L_k$-Lipschitz, then a $K$-stage network started at $\X^*$ returns $\|\X_K-\X^*\|\le\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big(\prod_{j=k}^{K}L_j\big)\|\mathbf{e}_k\|$, where $\mathbf{e}_k$ is $\mathbf{e}$ for the weights $\mathbf{W}_k$ of stage $k$.
\end{theorem}
The bounds follow from the singular values $b_i/(1+b_i^2)\le\tfrac12$ of $(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\top}$, $\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, and the nonexpansiveness of $T_k$ (\supp). Use the exact operator and the ground truth is a fixed point of every data step. Use an inexact one and each step pushes it away by an image-dependent amount that no fixed correction undoes. The exact step caps this push whatever the learned weights; the gradient step does not. Handing in the true operator of new data restores (a), which no learned operator can do.
\begin{corollary}[Border rule]
\label{cor:border}
If $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ differs from $\mathbf{A}$ only in the border rule of $\D_\theta$, then $(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$ is nonzero only on the low-resolution pixels whose kernel support reaches the padding, i.e., within $\lceil p/s\rceil$ pixels of the border for a $(2p+1)\times(2p+1)$ kernel.
\end{corollary}
In our benchmarks that touches only the first low-resolution row and column: 3\% of a $64\times64$ test image, but 12\% of the $16\times16$ training patches.

\subsection{Estimating the operators}
\label{sec:ident}
\begin{theorem}[Identifiability and stability]
\label{thm:ident}
Let $\D_\theta$ act identically on every band and $\R_\phi$ identically on every pixel, with kernels and responses that sum to one, and let $\mathbf{r}(\theta,\phi)=\D_\theta\YM-\R_\phi\YH$. (a) $\D_\theta\R_{\phi'}=\R_{\phi'}\D_\theta$, so for noise-free data generated with $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$ the criterion
\begin{equation}
J(\theta,\phi)=\|\mathbf{r}(\theta,\phi)\|^2=\|\D_\theta\YM-\R_\phi\YH\|^2
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
vanishes at $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$. (b) $J(\theta,\phi)=0$ if and only if $\X$ lies in the null space of $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}=\D_\theta\R_{\phi^*}-\R_\phi\D_{\theta^*}$, and $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}\neq0$ whenever $(\D_\theta,\R_\phi)\neq(\D_{\theta^*},\R_{\phi^*})$: two configurations are confused only on a proper subspace of images, such as spatially constant images (blur and offsets) or images with equal bands (response shift). (c) If the Jacobian $\mathbf{G}$ of $\mathbf{r}$ with respect to $(\theta,\phi)$ has full column rank at $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$, this point is an isolated zero of $J$ over the continuous parameters, and with observation noise the minimizer of $J$ moves, to first order, by $-\mathbf{G}^{+}\mathbf{n}$ with $\mathbf{n}=\D_{\theta^*}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}}-\R_{\phi^*}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}}$, hence by at most $\|\mathbf{n}\|/\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}[From noise to reconstruction]
\label{cor:chain}
Under (c), the exact data step with the estimated operator moves the ground truth by at most $\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\mathbf{A}'\|\,\|\mathbf{n}\|/(2\,\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G}))$ to first order, with $\mathbf{A}'$ the Jacobian of $(\theta,\phi)\mapsto\mathbf{A}_{\theta\phi}\X^*$.
\end{corollary}
Part (b) is exact for continuous families, not just unique on a finite grid. Part (c) puts a number on identifiability: the stability constant is $\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G})$, the conditioning of the estimate, and the corollary follows observation noise through the operator estimate into the reconstruction (proofs in \supp). $\theta$ can hold two blur widths and two grid offsets. For these five parameters $\mathbf{G}$ has full rank on the test images (condition number \qone{opan-chikusei-condnorm} with unit-norm columns on Chikusei). At 30~dB SNR, (c) predicts errors of \qone{opan-chikusei-crb30-off}~px for the offsets and \qone{opan-chikusei-crb30-sigma}~px for the blur width; we measure \qone{opan-chikusei-rmse30-off} and \qone{opan-chikusei-rmse30-sigma}~px, the excess coming from a second-order noise bias (\supp). HySure~\cite{simoes2015hysure} builds on the same relation (a). In practice we just search a grid for the minimum of $J$ (steps of $0.025\,\sigma_0$ and 1~nm); for a blur of arbitrary shape, least squares works too, because $\mathbf{r}$ is linear in the kernel (\cref{sec:cond}).

\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Stages.} ExactFuse (\cref{fig:arch}) runs $K=3$ HQS stages, starting from a bicubic upsampling $\X_0$ of $\YH$. Each stage first takes a data step with the exact operators. Usually that is the learned spectral step~\eqref{eq:klstep} below; for non-separable operators, and in the base model of \cref{sec:ablation}, we take the linearized step
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Z_k=\X_{k-1}&-s^2\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\D_\theta\X_{k-1}-\YH)\\
&-\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\X_{k-1}-\YM)
\end{split}
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
Each stage learns its step sizes $\alpha_k=\mathrm{softplus}(a_k)$ and $\beta_k=\mathrm{softplus}(b_k)$; the factor $s^2$ offsets the $1/s^2$ gain of $\D^{\top}\D$ on smooth images. The prior step adds a learned residual, $\X_k=\Z_k+\Delta_k$ with $(\Delta_k,\mathbf{F}_k)=\mathcal{P}_k([\Z_k,\R_\phi^{+}\YM,s^2\D^{\top}(\D\Z_k-\YH),\tfrac{B}{M}\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\Z_k-\YM)],\mathbf{F}_{k-1})$. $\R_\phi^{+}\YM$ holds the minimum-norm spectra in the $B$ hyperspectral bands that reproduce the HR-MSI ($\R_\phi\R_\phi^{+}=\mathbf{I}$, so they fix $\YM$ exactly). The last two inputs tell the prior where the estimate still disagrees with each observation. Since every input is in the hyperspectral bands, the prior is blind to how many multispectral bands there are and what their responses look like. $\mathbf{F}_{k-1}$ are the previous stage's decoder features (cross-stage memory). Each prior's last layer starts at zero, so training begins from plain HQS, which satisfies $\mathcal{P}_k(\X^*)=\mathbf{0}$ in \cref{thm:mismatch}(c). Operators run in 32-bit precision.

\paragraph{Learned spectral data step.} In the basis of \cref{thm:kron} the exact step scales each component of the data residual by $1/(1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b)$, the filter of a white Gaussian prior. But natural images are not white, and noise hits some components harder than others. So two small networks modulate the gains:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Z_k&=\X_{k-1}+\mathcal{Q}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{G}_k)\odot\bm{\Gamma}_k\big),\\
\Gamma_{k,bij}&=\frac{\exp\!\big(\ln2\,\tanh(u_k(\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j)+v_k(\nu_b))\big)}{1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b},
\end{split}
\label{eq:klstep}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{G}_k=\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\YH-\D_\theta\X_{k-1})+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\YM-\R_\phi\X_{k-1})$, and $u_k$, $v_k$ are two-layer perceptrons fed with the log-eigenvalues (plus the observation code defined below). Zero initialization makes training start from~\eqref{eq:kronstep}. The gain factorizes over space and spectrum, so each stage evaluates the perceptrons $HW+B$ times, not $BHW$.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:kl}
For any $u_k,v_k$: (a) with exact operators and noise-free data, $\X^*$ is a fixed point of~\eqref{eq:klstep}; (b) the step is nonexpansive; (c) with an inexact operator it moves $\X^*$ by at most $\|\mathbf{e}\|$, twice the bound~\eqref{eq:bound} of the exact step.
\end{proposition}
The gain stays within $\tfrac12$ to $2$ times the exact gain, which keeps every factor of the step in $(-1,1]$ (\supp). The learned step can thus decide how far to trust each observed component, yet it cannot move a correct estimate or blow up errors.

\paragraph{Prior.} Each prior is a three-level U-Net of Restormer blocks~\cite{zamir2022restormer} (\cref{fig:arch}b,c), whose transposed attention builds its map across channels at a cost linear in the pixel count. ExactFuse has \qone{puformer-params}~M parameters for Chikusei, nearly all in the three priors. The prior architecture is not a contribution; \cref{sec:ablation} shows a convolutional mixer does about as well.

\paragraph{Training over a family of observation models.} Train with one operator pair on clean data and the priors adapt to that blur, that response and zero noise. In ExactFuse the operators enter only through the data steps, so for each batch we draw a blur width $\sigma\sim\mathcal{U}[0.5,2]\sigma_0$, a spectral shift $\delta\sim\mathcal{U}[-12,12]$~nm and, with probability 0.5, noise at an SNR from $\mathcal{U}[30,50]$~dB, simulate the batch with $(\D_\sigma,\R_\delta)$ and hand the same operators to the data steps. At test time they are either given or estimated with~\eqref{eq:J}. Following HQS, we also condition on the noise. A code $\mathbf{c}=(\sigma/\sigma_0-1,\delta/12,\log_{10}n_{\mathrm{H}}+2,\log_{10}n_{\mathrm{M}}+2)$, in which the relative noise level $n$ of each observation is estimated as the median absolute deviation of its second differences, sets the step sizes $\alpha_k=\mathrm{softplus}(a_k+\mathbf{u}_k^{\top}\mathbf{c})$ and $\beta_k=\mathrm{softplus}(b_k+\mathbf{v}_k^{\top}\mathbf{c})$ and modulates the prior features through FiLM, much like the hyper-parameter module of USRNet~\cite{zhang2020usrnet}.

\paragraph{Loss.} We train on $\|\X_K-\X\|_1+0.1\sum_{k<K}\|\X_k-\X\|_1+0.05\,\mathrm{SAM}(\X_K,\X)+0.1\,(1-\mathrm{SSIM}(\X_K,\X))$ with AdamW, and test the exponential moving average of the weights.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Data.} Chikusei~\cite{yokoya2016chikusei} (128 bands, 363--1018~nm) is paired with the eight WorldView-2 responses and Pavia Centre~\cite{gamba2004pavia} (92 bands) with the four IKONOS ones. Split sizes follow~\cite{chen2026twostage}, 8 and 4 test images of $256\times256$ pixels, at fixed, disjoint locations given in \supp. CAVE~\cite{yasuma2010cave} (20 training, 12 test scenes) and Harvard~\cite{chakrabarti2011harvard} (30 and 20 images) have 31 bands and use a Nikon RGB response. Every pair follows Wald's protocol~\cite{vivone2015critical}: both observations are simulated from the reference with~\eqref{eq:obs}, each training crop on its own. At $\times8$ and $\times16$ the blur is a $(2s-1)\times(2s-1)$ Gaussian with $\sigma=s/2$.

\paragraph{Baselines and budget.} We compare against GSA~\cite{aiazzi2007gsa} and \qone{n-retrained-word} networks: \qone{retrained-list}. Each comes from its authors' code; we changed only the band counts and the scale factor (commits in \supp). Every method, ours too, trains for 4~h on one Tesla T4 with the same $64\times64$ patches, batch size (8), augmentation, EMA weights and validation-only model selection. SSR-NET converges sooner and gets 1.5~h. The published networks keep their own learning rates, with an L1 loss. A single script computes all metrics: PSNR (peak set to the dataset maximum), SSIM, the spectral angle SAM (degrees) and ERGAS; Q2n, CC, SCC and RMSE are in \supp.

\subsection{Where the margin comes from}
\label{sec:nullsplit}

We strip ExactFuse down one component at a time (\supp). One U-Net prior on the bicubic image and the HR-MSI, with a single stage and no data steps, already gets \qone{gain-chikusei-abl-unet-psnr}~dB on Chikusei and \qone{gain-pavia-abl-unet-psnr}~dB on Pavia Centre, ahead of every retrained network. So most of the margin is owed to this residual multiscale prior and its training recipe, not to the transformer blocks; the exact data steps add \qone{gain-chikusei-abl-unet-gap} and \qone{gain-pavia-abl-unet-gap}~dB. The recipe is not the whole story, though. Under a shared recipe ExactFuse still leads MIMFormer, by \qone{recipe-chikusei-ours-gap} and \qone{recipe-pavia-ours-gap}~dB with ours and \qone{recipe-chikusei-base-gap} and \qone{recipe-pavia-base-gap}~dB with the baselines'.

The null space of \cref{thm:kron}(c) offers another angle. We split each method's error into a part in the observed subspace $\mathrm{row}(\D)+\mathrm{row}(\R)$, which contradicts the observations, and a null-space part that only a prior can shrink. Bicubic interpolation and GSA go wrong mostly in the observed subspace: on Chikusei just \qone{null-chikusei-bicubic-share}\% and \qone{null-chikusei-gsa-share}\% of their squared error lies in the null space. ExactFuse's observed error (column Obs.\ of \cref{tab:bench}) is \qone{null-chikusei-obs-ratio-best} times below the best retrained network's on Chikusei (\qone{null-chikusei-obs-best}) and \qone{null-pavia-obs-ratio-best} times below on Pavia Centre. Cancelling only MIMFormer's observed error would lift its Chikusei PSNR by \qone{null-chikusei-consistency-gain}~dB; the remaining \qone{null-chikusei-prior-gain}~dB of our margin come from a better null-space estimate. We credit the first part to the exact data steps, the second to the prior.

\subsection{What the exact operator does}
\label{sec:ablation}
\qtab{cvpr_ablation}

Every variant in \cref{tab:ablation} is retrained from scratch on the same budget; the top block builds ExactFuse up from the base model. The closed-form step with learned gains and the HR-MSI residual input each make the output more consistent with the LR-HSI (\qone{kcab-kl-consH} and \qone{kcab-mres-consH} against \qone{kcab-base-consH}~dB on Chikusei) at nearly the same PSNR. Together, with all inputs in the hyperspectral bands, they shift the PSNR by \qone{kcab-pf-delta} and \qone{cab-pf-delta}~dB (Chikusei, Pavia Centre) and lift consistency to \qone{kcab-pf-consH}~dB. A learned gate that keeps the prior inside the null space hurts PSNR (\qone{kcab-gate-vspf} and \qone{cab-gate-vspf}~dB), so we leave it out.\par Drop the data steps and ExactFuse loses \qone{kcab-ng-nophys-absvspf} and \qone{cab-ng-nophys-absvspf}~dB, while LR-HSI consistency falls from \qone{kcab-pf-consH} to \qone{kcab-ng-nophys-consH}~dB. Swapping the closed form for a gradient step keeps the PSNR but cuts consistency to \qone{kcab-ng-grad-consH}~dB. The learned gains, the residual input and $\R^{+}\YM$ each change the PSNR by at most \qone{ab-small-max}~dB; one stage, a convolutional mixer or no cross-stage memory by at most \qone{ab-arch-max}~dB; a zero border by \qone{kcab-ng-zeros-absvspf} and \qone{cab-ng-zeros-absvspf}~dB\qoneif{cab-ng-learned-psnr}{; and learned operators by \qone{kcab-ng-learned-absvspf} and \qone{cab-ng-learned-absvspf}~dB}. In the base model a zero border or a learned blur and response cost \qone{kabl-zeros-absdelta} and \qone{kabl-learned-absdelta}~dB on Chikusei and reproduce the LR-HSI roughly 3~dB worse, just as \cref{thm:mismatch} predicts.

Change the blur and the gaps widen (\supp). Given the true blur, the exact base model loses at most \qone{kabl-full-swaploss}~dB on Chikusei and the zero-border model \qone{kabl-zeros-swaploss}~dB; the learned-operator model, which cannot be swapped, loses \qone{kabl-learned-fixedloss}~dB.

\subsection{One network for a family of observation models}
\label{sec:cond}

\Cref{tab:cond} puts networks trained over the operator family under conditions outside the training protocol. Inside the family the estimate of \cref{thm:ident} is accurate: across all Chikusei test images and conditions, 30~dB SNR included, its worst errors are \qone{cond-chikusei-sigerr-max}~px in blur width and \qone{cond-chikusei-shifterr-max}~nm in spectral shift. Family training makes the network robust to noise and to combined changes. At 40--30~dB SNR ExactFuse's mean Chikusei PSNR climbs from \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-bench-puformer-fixed} to \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-cond-ng-estimated}~dB, against \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-cond-mimformer-fixed}~dB for MIMFormer trained on the same randomized data, though it pays with a lower PSNR under the training operator (\qone{cond-chikusei-nominal-cond-ng-estimated} against \qone{chikusei4-psnr}~dB). A single network trained on random responses also copes with seven real sensors it never saw. On Chikusei it is \qone{fam-chikusei-vsmim-min}--\qone{fam-chikusei-vsmim-max}~dB above MIMFormer trained the same way, and \qone{fam-chikusei-own-cost}~dB below ExactFuse trained for WorldView-2. Trained at $\times$4 and $\times$8, a network leads the best retrained one at the unseen $\times$16 by \qone{scl-chikusei-x16-gain}~dB (\supp).

Outside the family the parametric estimate breaks down: with an anisotropic or defocus blur ExactFuse falls to \qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-ng-estimated}~dB on Chikusei. Since $\mathbf{r}$ is linear in the kernel, a kernel of any shape can be fit by least squares instead. ExactFuse then gets \qone{np-chikusei-ng-kernel} and \qone{np-pavia-ng-kernel}~dB under these blurs, on par with the true kernel (\qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-ng-oracle} and \qone{cond-pavia-kernel-cond-ng-oracle}~dB) and ahead of MIMFormer trained on the same randomized data (\qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-mimformer-fixed} and \qone{cond-pavia-kernel-cond-mimformer-fixed}~dB). Misregistration is harder. Shift the HR-MSI by half a pixel and GSA and every network trained on registered pairs lose more than 15~dB. Registering the HR-MSI first, with the offset estimated from the pair, wins most of it back (\supp). For a sharp half-pixel shift, as a sensor would record it, ExactFuse reaches \qone{reg-chikusei-puformer-reg_msi-sharp05}~dB on Chikusei (MIMFormer \qone{reg-chikusei-mimformer-reg_msi-sharp05}~dB, GSA \qone{reg-chikusei-puformer-gsa_reg-sharp05}~dB).

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~Reference-based results rely on simulated observations (Wald's protocol~\cite{vivone2015critical}). On real NEON data (\supp) ExactFuse is again the most accurate at reduced resolution (\qone{neon-puformer-psnr} against \qone{neon-mimformer-psnr}~dB for MIMFormer)\qoneif{sjer-gain}{ and leads by \qone{sjer-gain}~dB on an unseen site}, but there its operator variants differ by under 0.1~dB, as the fitted camera response is only approximate; the sensor tests pair real responses with simulated data. (ii)~Exact operators help only if the family contains the true operator. Spatially varying blurs escape even the least-squares kernel estimate, which costs \qone{np-chikusei-ng-snr30-cost}--\qone{np-pavia-ng-snr30-cost}~dB at 30~dB SNR, and registration still leaves a misregistration loss of several decibels\qoneif{reg-chikusei-m2dtnf-msi_frame-sharp05}{ (the registration-aware M2DTN~\cite{dong2024m2dtn}, retrained on misregistered pairs and applied without registration, outperforms it for bilinear shifts on Pavia Centre, \qone{reg-pavia-m2dtnf-msi_frame-pure} vs.\ \qone{reg-pavia-puformer-reg_msi-pure}~dB, \supp)}\qoneelse{reg-chikusei-m2dtnf-msi_frame-sharp05}{ (registration-aware networks such as IR\&ArF~\cite{qu2025irarf} were not retrained)}. Non-separable kernels lose the closed form. Corrupted pixels in a four-band HR-MSI are tolerated, but only a model-free detector localizes them (\supp). (iii)~The Chikusei and Pavia Centre test sets are small; three seeds, bootstrap intervals (\supp), the pooled test, and CAVE and Harvard back up the comparison. (iv)~ExactFuse takes \qone{pc-total-s}~s per $256\times256$ pair on a T4, roughly twice MIMFormer's time (\supp).

Exact separable operators with the right border rule give unfolded fusion a closed-form data step, an explicit null space, bounded error under operator mismatch, and identifiable operators. ExactFuse beats \qone{n-retrained-word} retrained networks, mostly thanks to its prior and training, and adapts to new operators, sensors and scale factors. Spatially varying operators and real paired references are still open problems. We will release the code.
