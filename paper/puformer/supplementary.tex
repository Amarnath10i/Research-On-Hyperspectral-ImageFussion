\section{Training Stages and the Exact Border}
\label{sec:s-stages}
\textbf{Chikusei.} The final Chikusei model was obtained in two runs: a first run from scratch with zero-padded operators and the L1 losses only, and a fine-tuning run with the exact reflect-border operators and the full loss. Table~\ref{tab:stages} traces the path from the first run to the final model. Evaluating the first-run weights with the exact reflect-border operator, without retraining, drops the validation PSNR from 58.60 to 49.52~dB. The prior had learned to undo the error that the zero-padded operator introduces at the border, so with the exact operator it over-corrects there; the effect is strongest on the small $64\times64$ validation patches, where border pixels are a large fraction of the image. Fine-tuning removes this within 2000 iterations (58.60~dB validation) and ends at 58.72~dB. The consistency of the reconstruction with its inputs rises from 76.7 to 85.5~dB for the LR-HSI and from 75.0 to 89.0~dB for the HR-MSI. Fig.~\ref{fig:training} shows the validation curves; validation PSNR stops improving after about 44{,}000 fine-tuning iterations, and the test PSNR stays between 58.13 and 58.15~dB from iteration 26{,}000 onward.

\textbf{Pavia Centre.} The Pavia Centre model was trained from scratch with the exact operators and the full loss. Validation PSNR stops improving after \pav{best-it} iterations (Fig.~\ref{fig:trainpavia}), and those weights are tested; the test PSNR, logged but never used for selection, peaks at \pav{test-peak}~dB at \pav{test-peak-it} iterations, against \pav{test-at-best}~dB for the selected weights. The reconstruction reproduces its inputs to within \pav{cons-H}~dB (LR-HSI) and \pav{cons-M}~dB (HR-MSI).

\section{Robustness to Degradation Mismatch and Noise}
\label{sec:s-robust}
Published methods are usually trained and tested with the same degradation. We test the final models on inputs made with a different blur or a shifted spectral response, and with Gaussian noise added to both observations (Table~\ref{tab:robust}, Fig.~\ref{fig:robust}). Because the operators are explicit, ExactFuse can also be given the true test-time operator without retraining (``swap''). On Chikusei, a blur mismatch costs 3.9--7.5~dB with the training operator. With the true blur swapped in, the loss is at most 0.26~dB for $\sigma$ between 1.5 and 3.0; with the zero-border operators of the first run, the same swap recovered only to 55.2--57.1~dB. A shifted spectral response is not compensated by the swap, which lowers the PSNR by a further 0.5~dB: the priors, trained with one response, rely on it beyond the data step. Noise is the main weakness of a model trained on noise-free data; ExactFuse still exceeds GSA by 10.0~dB at 40~dB SNR and by 2.8~dB at 30~dB SNR. On Pavia Centre the blur behaves the same way: the training operator loses \pav{blur-fixed-loss-min}--\pav{blur-fixed-loss-max}~dB and the swapped-in true operator at most \pav{blur-swap-loss-max}~dB. Unlike on Chikusei, swapping in a shifted IKONOS response raises the PSNR slightly (by 0.2--0.3~dB), perhaps because an 8~nm shift is small relative to the 66--96~nm IKONOS bands, and the margins over GSA under noise are larger (\pav{noise40-margin}, \pav{noise35-margin} and \pav{noise30-margin}~dB at 40, 35 and 30~dB SNR).

\section{Q2n on Chikusei}
\label{sec:s-q2n}
Q2n normalizes every band of every $32\times32$ block by the ground truth's own mean and standard deviation in that block, so an error that is small in absolute terms counts heavily in a band that is nearly flat within a block. Table~\ref{tab:q2n} and Fig.~\ref{fig:q2n} decompose the Q2n of the final model (\QtwonVthree). If the eight ultraviolet bands (363--404~nm) were reconstructed exactly, Q2n would rise to \QtwonExactUV; if the last 24 bands were exact as well, to \QtwonExactUVNIR. In the worst 10\% of blocks, the error energy of these eight bands equals \RelErrUV\ of the ground truth's local variance, against at most \RelErrMaxOther\ for any other group of eight bands. The fine detail of these bands cannot be predicted from the inputs: a least-squares injection of the multispectral detail, fitted on the test ground truth itself, reduces the band-1 error only from \OracleBicA\ to \OracleLinA~DN, while ExactFuse reaches \OracleModelA~DN, and ExactFuse is more accurate than this oracle in every band we checked.

Q2n is therefore nearly insensitive to overall accuracy on these test images: blending our reconstruction toward bicubic interpolation lowers the PSNR from \CalibPsnrHi\ to \CalibPsnrMid~dB but Q2n only from \CalibQHi\ to \CalibQMid\ (Fig.~\ref{fig:q2n}, right). The values reported in~\cite{chen2026twostage} do not follow PSNR in this way. Between 52.2 and 53.6~dB they range from 0.9832 (DCT) to 0.9963 (DDIF); of the 16 methods within the PSNR range of our curve, 11 lie above it and 5 below. U2Net, for example, is listed at 0.9928 at 50.55~dB, a value our implementation reaches only near 57.6~dB. We conclude that the Q2n values of~\cite{chen2026twostage} were most likely computed with a different implementation, normalization or test region, and that they are not directly comparable with ours.

\section{SAM and Q2n on Pavia Centre}
\label{sec:s-pavia}
The Q2n shortfall on Pavia Centre comes from one test image, which also has the largest SAM (Table~\ref{tab:perimage}, Fig.~\ref{fig:sampavia}). On images 1, 3 and 4, Q2n is 0.9987--0.9991, at or above the best reported value, but SAM is still 1.55--1.68$^\circ$ (mean 1.60$^\circ$), above the best reported 1.454$^\circ$: the water image explains the Q2n gap, but only part of the SAM gap. Image 2 contains a stretch of the river Ticino: \pav{water-2}\% of its pixels have a positive normalized difference water index (green against near infrared), against \pav{water-1}, \pav{water-3} and \pav{water-4}\% in the other images, and its SAM is 2.72$^\circ$ and its Q2n 0.904. Water reflects little light, so its spectra are dominated by sensor noise that neither observation carries. The per-band noise that we estimate from the ground truth alone makes an angle of \pav{noise-floor-dark}$^\circ$ with the noise-free spectrum in the darkest tenth of the pixels, against \pav{noise-floor-bright}$^\circ$ in the brightest tenth. Over water ExactFuse's SAM is \pav{sam-water}$^\circ$, over all other pixels \pav{sam-nowater}$^\circ$, and the darkest 10\% of the pixels account for \pav{sam-dark10}\% of its mean SAM. The same pixels make image 2 nearly flat within many $32\times32$ blocks, where Q2n divides the error by a very small local variance; image 2 alone carries 97\% of the Q2n deficit $\sum_i(1-\mathrm{Q2n}_i)$ of the four images.

\section{Further Results of the Long-Trained Models}
\label{sec:s-more}
Table~\ref{tab:ours} lists the long-trained ExactFuse models of the comparisons with reported results, together with bicubic interpolation and GSA, under every PSNR and SSIM definition in use, and Table~\ref{tab:perimage} gives the results of every test image. Chikusei images 5--8 are darker (their maximum is 0.36--0.47 of the dataset maximum), which lowers their strict SSIM but not their RMSE. Fig.~\ref{fig:visual} shows Chikusei image 7, the hardest test image, and Fig.~\ref{fig:visualpavia} Pavia Centre image \pav{visual-tile}, the hardest there. The errors of bicubic interpolation and GSA follow every field boundary and road (mean absolute errors of 164.7 and 64.8~DN), whereas both ExactFuse models keep the error small and nearly uniform over the image (11.5 and 11.0~DN), apart from a few isolated pixels.

Fig.~\ref{fig:bandwise} shows the error of every band. ExactFuse is more accurate than GSA in all 128 Chikusei bands, and the final model improves on the first run in every band, most at the red edge (700--760~nm), where the mean band RMSE falls from 7.9 to 4.2~DN (from 9.8 to 1.1~DN at 724~nm). The error is largest in the first eight bands (363--404~nm), which lie below the first WorldView-2 band and therefore receive no high-resolution detail from the multispectral image, and in the last bands near 1000~nm, where the sensor's signal-to-noise ratio drops.

\section{Related Work with Full References}
The main paper cites only the compared methods, the datasets and its closest references; this section gives its related work with all references, and the references of further details of the main paper.
\subsection{Model-based fusion}
Component substitution methods such as GSA~\cite{aiazzi2007gsa} inject multispectral detail into the upsampled hyperspectral bands. Unmixing-based methods such as CNMF~\cite{yokoya2012cnmf} factor both observations with shared endmembers, and subspace methods such as HySure~\cite{simoes2015hysure} regularize the HR-HSI within a low-dimensional spectral subspace, whose dimension can be estimated with HySime~\cite{bioucas2008hysime}. FUSE~\cite{wei2015fuse} solves the resulting quadratic problem in closed form through a Sylvester equation. These methods use the forward model explicitly but rely on hand-crafted priors.

\subsection{Supervised deep fusion}
SSR-NET~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet} and GuidedNet~\cite{ran2023guidednet} learn a direct mapping from the two observations. Transformers model long-range spatial and spectral dependencies: PSRT~\cite{deng2023psrt} uses pyramid shuffle-and-reshuffle attention, MIMO-SST~\cite{fang2024mimosst} multi-input multi-output supervision, LGCT~\cite{he2024lgct} local--global collaboration, DSPNet~\cite{shan2023dspnet} a dual spatial--spectral pyramid, SSDT~\cite{fu2025ssdt} dilated attention, and MIMFormer~\cite{li2024mimformer}, AMSF-Net~\cite{liu2025amsf}, ASMNet~\cite{liu2025asmnet} and DPFormer~\cite{liu2026dpformer} multiscale spectral--spatial designs. Recent work also studies state-space models~\cite{peng2024fusionmamba,huang2026hdgmamba}, mixture-of-experts routing~\cite{xiao2026ramoe} and modality interaction~\cite{huo2026mian}. General image-fusion frameworks such as U2Net~\cite{peng2023u2net} and OTPNet~\cite{yu2025otpnet} have also been applied to this task.

\subsection{Model-guided and unfolded networks}
Algorithm unrolling~\cite{gregor2010lista,monga2021unrolling,zhang2018istanet} turns the iterations of an optimization method into network stages. MHF-Net~\cite{xie2022mhfnet}, MoG-DCN~\cite{dong2021mogdcn} and DHIF-Net~\cite{huang2022dhif} unfold fusion-specific solvers, MHF-Net and DHIF-Net with operators learned as convolution layers, DUNET~\cite{fang2024dunet} adds transformer priors for unregistered inputs, IR\&ArF~\cite{qu2025irarf} unfolds the fusion of unregistered images at arbitrary resolution with a learned registration, HMPNet~\cite{tian2024hmpnet} extends unfolding to three modalities, and an information-aware transformer-based unfolding network (ITU-Net)~\cite{sun2025iatun}, a mutual-guidance unfolding network (SMGU)~\cite{yan2025smgu}, the perceptive spectral transformer unfolding network (PSTUN)~\cite{wang2025pstun}, which also handles arbitrary scales, and the cross-domain-aware unfolding transformer CaFormer~\cite{cao2026caformer} strengthen the learned proximal steps with transformer priors. ExactFuse belongs to this family, and none of its prior's components is new: passing features from one stage to the next follows memory-augmented unfolding~\cite{song2021madun}, spectral-wise attention inside an unfolded network appears in DAUHST~\cite{cai2022dauhst}, and the transposed attention is that of Restormer~\cite{zamir2022restormer}. A single unfolded network that takes the blur kernel as an input, and therefore handles kernels it was not trained on, was shown for single-image super-resolution by USRNet~\cite{zhang2020usrnet}.

\subsection{Boundary conditions}
In classical deconvolution, an image border handled differently by the model and by the data causes ringing that spreads inward, and reflexive (Neumann) boundary conditions, i.e., reflect padding, are a standard remedy~\cite{ng1999neumann,reeves2005boundary,hansen2006deblurring}. In iterative reconstruction, a back-projection that is not the exact adjoint of the forward model changes the point to which the iteration converges~\cite{zeng2000unmatched,savanier2021adjoint}, and in convolutional networks zero padding is itself a source of spatial bias near the border~\cite{alsallakh2021pad}. In unfolded fusion networks the operators are either learned or implemented with the zero padding of convolution layers, while benchmark data are simulated with other border rules; to our knowledge, the effect of this mismatch has not been examined. Our contribution is on this side: an unfolded network whose operators, border included, are exactly those of the simulation, and an ablation that separates the exact operator from a zero-border and a learned operator under the same budget.

\subsection{Generative fusion}
Diffusion models have been conditioned on the two observations (DDIF~\cite{cao2024ddif}), constrained by the observation model during sampling (CDPS~\cite{xu2026cdps}), restricted to a learned subspace (ARGS-Diff~\cite{zhu2025argsdiff}) or combined with differential attention in a two-stage conditioning scheme~\cite{chen2026twostage}. Null-space formulations~\cite{wang2023ddnm} make the data-consistency part of the reconstruction exact. The two-stage conditional diffusion model of Chen~\textit{et al.}~\cite{chen2026twostage} holds the best published Chikusei $\times$4 results in our survey and is our main reference; its comparison also includes the correlation-aware network CLSNet~\cite{yang2026clsnet} and the dual cross transformer (DCTransformer)~\cite{ma2024reciprocal}, denoted DCT in their table.

\subsection{Further references of the main paper}
The spectral responses are those of WorldView-2~\cite{digitalglobe2014srf} and IKONOS~\cite{dial2003ikonos}; the operator estimate uses the relation of HySure~\cite{simoes2015hysure}, separable operators also appear in STEREO~\cite{kanatsoulis2018stereo}, and the true border rule of real images is unknown~\cite{almeida2013boundaries}. ExactFuse starts from the bicubic upsampling~\cite{keys1981cubic}, runs its priors in mixed precision~\cite{micikevicius2018mixed}, uses a U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015unet}, deep supervision~\cite{lee2015dsn}, FiLM modulation~\cite{perez2018film}, a noise estimate after~\cite{donoho1994ideal}, randomized degradations as in blind super-resolution~\cite{zhang2018srmd,zhang2021bsrgan} and the self-ensemble of~\cite{lim2017edsr}. Pairs follow Wald's protocol~\cite{wald1997fusion}. Training uses AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}, cosine decay~\cite{loshchilov2017sgdr}, distributed data parallelism~\cite{li2020ddp} and, for some baselines, gradient checkpointing~\cite{chen2016checkpoint}; the ablation replaces the token mixer with depth-wise convolution~\cite{chollet2017xception} or window attention~\cite{liu2021swin}. The metrics are SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim}, SAM~\cite{yuhas1992sam}, ERGAS~\cite{wald2000ergas}, Q2n~\cite{garzelli2009q2n} as in the pansharpening toolbox~\cite{vivone2015critical,deng2022machine}, and SCC~\cite{zhou1998scc}; significance uses the Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon1945}. The methods tabulated from~\cite{chen2026twostage} in the comparison with reported results are GSA~\cite{aiazzi2007gsa}, CNMF~\cite{yokoya2012cnmf}, FUSE~\cite{wei2015fuse}, U2Net~\cite{peng2023u2net}, PSRT~\cite{deng2023psrt}, DCT~\cite{ma2024reciprocal}, DDIF~\cite{cao2024ddif}, MIMFormer~\cite{li2024mimformer}, MIMO-SST~\cite{fang2024mimosst}, OTPNet~\cite{yu2025otpnet}, SMGU~\cite{yan2025smgu}, AMSF~\cite{liu2025amsf}, SSDT~\cite{fu2025ssdt}, ASMNet~\cite{liu2025asmnet}, DPFormer~\cite{liu2026dpformer} and CLSNet~\cite{yang2026clsnet}.
