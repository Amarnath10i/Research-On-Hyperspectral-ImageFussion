\title{ExactFuse: Closed-Form Unfolding with Exact Operators for Sensor-Independent Hyperspectral and Multispectral Image Fusion}

\author{\cvprauthors}   % hidden in review mode

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Deep unfolding networks for hyperspectral and multispectral image fusion alternate data steps with learned priors, but their blur, downsampling and spectral-response operators are usually learned or zero-padded, so they differ from the operators that produced the data. We study what exact operators bring to such a network. With a separable blur and the true border rule, the data step has a closed form in a product eigenbasis, and the null space of fusion, the part of the image that no observation constrains, is explicit. An exact step keeps the ground truth fixed, an inexact one moves it by an amount that we bound, and blur, spectral response and sub-pixel grid offsets can be estimated from the two observations. Our network, ExactFuse, takes this closed-form step with learned spectral gains, gives its prior inputs in the hyperspectral bands only, so that one network can serve any multispectral sensor whose response is known, and is trained over a family of observation models whose operators it estimates at test time. Against \qone{n-retrained-word} published networks retrained from their authors' code under one protocol and budget, ExactFuse has the highest PSNR on Chikusei and Pavia Centre, by \qone{chikusei4-gain} and \qone{pavia4-gain}~dB, and in \qone{gen-psnr-wins} of \qone{gen-complete} further settings up to $\times16$, and it reproduces the low-resolution input \qone{chikusei4-cons-gain}~dB more accurately than the strongest of them. A prior without data steps already beats every baseline, so much of the margin comes from the prior and its training; the exact operators make the result consistent with its inputs, replaceable at test time and robust to changed blurs, responses and noise.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Hyperspectral cameras record hundreds of narrow bands at a coarse spatial resolution, and multispectral cameras record a few broad bands at a fine one. Fusing the two images gives a high-resolution hyperspectral image (HR-HSI)~\cite{vivone2025deep}. Most recent methods are deep networks trained on simulated pairs: convolutional networks~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet}, transformers~\cite{deng2023psrt,ma2024reciprocal,li2024mimformer}, state-space models~\cite{pei2026s3o} and diffusion models~\cite{chen2026twostage}.

Deep unfolding networks~\cite{xie2022mhfnet,dong2021mogdcn,huang2022dhif,yan2025smgu} unroll an iterative solver of the inverse problem: each stage takes a data step, which reduces the mismatch with the two observations, and applies a learned prior. The data step needs the blur, downsampling and spectral response, which most unfolding networks learn as convolutions or fix with zero padding, although the benchmark data are simulated with a known blur and another border rule; even the ground truth then fails the network's own consistency check. Each network also serves a single sensor pair.

Evaluation is a second problem: splits, responses, normalizations and metric code differ between papers~\cite{vivone2025deep}, so PSRT~\cite{deng2023psrt}, for example, is reported at 44.60~dB PSNR on Chikusei $\times$4 in~\cite{pei2026s3o} and at 50.67~dB in~\cite{chen2026twostage}, and copied numbers cannot rank methods.

This paper asks what exact operators bring to an unfolded fusion network. We write fusion as maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation with parametric operators, unroll half-quadratic splitting (HQS)~\cite{zhang2020usrnet} and study the resulting data step. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item Our central contribution is a theory of exact operators in unfolded fusion that links observation noise to reconstruction error. With a separable blur and the true border rule, the data step has a closed form and the fusion null space is explicit (88\% of the unknowns on Chikusei). The operators are identifiable from the observation pair over continuous parameters, with an exact condition for when two sensor configurations cannot be told apart and a stability bound under noise, and an operator error moves the reconstruction by an amount bounded independently of the learned weights (\cref{sec:theory}). None of this applies to networks with learned or zero-padded operators.
\item ExactFuse, an unfolded network with exact and replaceable operators, a learned spectral data step that keeps these guarantees, inputs in the hyperspectral bands, so that one set of weights serves any multispectral sensor whose response is known, and training over a family of observation models with the operators estimated at test time (\cref{sec:method}). Its building blocks are standard and not claimed.
\item An equal-budget benchmark of \qone{n-retrained-word} published networks, retrained from their authors' code, and a null-space split of the error that shows where each method fails (\cref{sec:exp}). It also shows where ExactFuse's margin comes from: much of it from the prior and its training, which keep a lead under equal recipes, while the exact operators make the result consistent with its inputs and matter most when the operator changes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Deep fusion networks} map the two observations directly to the HR-HSI with convolutions~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet}, transformers~\cite{deng2023psrt,ma2024reciprocal,li2024mimformer,fang2024mimosst,yang2026clsnet}, general fusion backbones~\cite{peng2023u2net}, state-space models~\cite{pei2026s3o} or diffusion~\cite{chen2026twostage}, without using the observation model. SSA~\cite{liang2026ssa} slices its convolution kernels to the number of bands and reconstructs at arbitrary scales, so one model serves several sensors; it does not model their responses.

\paragraph{Unfolding and operator knowledge.} Algorithm unrolling~\cite{monga2021unrolling} turns solver iterations into network stages. MHF-Net~\cite{xie2022mhfnet}, MoG-DCN~\cite{dong2021mogdcn} and DHIF-Net~\cite{huang2022dhif} unfold fusion solvers with learned or approximate operators, later networks strengthen the prior~\cite{yan2025smgu} or the proximal update~\cite{yu2025otpnet}, and IR\&ArF~\cite{qu2025irarf} unfolds the fusion of unregistered images with a learned registration. USRNet~\cite{zhang2020usrnet} gives the blur kernel of single-image super-resolution to a closed-form data step under periodic borders, and blind methods train on randomized degradations~\cite{zhang2021bsrgan}; we carry this to fusion, with two operators and the true border rule.

\paragraph{Closed forms and separable operators.} FUSE~\cite{wei2015fuse} solves the quadratic fusion problem with a Sylvester equation under periodic borders, and tensor methods~\cite{li2018cstf,kanatsoulis2018stereo,prevost2020scott} use separable degradations to prove recoverability of low-rank models. Our closed form holds for the reflect border of the simulation and is a differentiable layer whose operators change per image; its learned filters resemble deep Wiener deconvolution~\cite{dong2020wiener}, bounded so that the step stays nonexpansive.

\paragraph{Range and null space.} A split of the reconstruction into a part fixed by the measurements and a null-space part underlies range--null-space networks~\cite{chen2020decomposition}, back-projection improves the consistency of pansharpening~\cite{vicinanza2014consistency}, and an adjoint that does not match the data biases tomographic reconstruction~\cite{zeng2000unmatched}. With exact separable operators the projection has a closed form (\cref{thm:kron}), which we use to split every method's error.

\section{Exact operators in unfolded fusion}
\label{sec:theory}
\subsection{Observation model and unfolding}
\label{sec:hqs}
Let $\X\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times H\times W}$ be the HR-HSI. The two observations are
\begin{equation}
\YH=\D_\theta\X+\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}},\qquad \YM=\R_\phi\X+\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}},
\label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
where $\D_\theta$ applies to every band reflect padding, a Gaussian blur of width $\theta$ and decimation by $s$ starting at index zero, so $\YH\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times h\times w}$ with $h=H/s$, $w=W/s$; $\R_\phi$ applies to every pixel the $M$ spectral responses of the multispectral sensor, shifted along wavelength by $\phi$~nm; and $\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}},\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}}$ are white Gaussian noise with standard deviations $\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}},\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}$. The benchmarks use $s=4$, a $7\times7$ blur with $\theta=\sigma_0=2$, $\phi=0$ and no noise~\cite{chen2026twostage}. We implement $\D_\theta$ and its exact adjoint, in which the adjoint of reflect padding adds the padded margins back onto the pixels they were copied from (\supp); the adjoint test $\langle\D\X,\mathbf{Y}\rangle=\langle\X,\D^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\rangle$ holds to $10^{-13}$ in double precision.

With a prior $p(\X)\propto\exp(-\lambda\Phi(\X))$, the MAP estimate minimizes $\tfrac{1}{2\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2}\|\D_\theta\X-\YH\|^2+\tfrac{1}{2\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2}\|\R_\phi\X-\YM\|^2+\lambda\Phi(\X)$. HQS couples $\X$ to an auxiliary image $\Z$ with a penalty $\tfrac{\mu_k}{2}\|\Z-\X\|^2$ and alternates a data step and a prior step, $\X_k=\mathrm{prox}_{(\lambda/\mu_k)\Phi}(\Z_k)$. With $\alpha_k=1/(\mu_k\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2)$ and $\beta_k=1/(\mu_k\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2)$ the data step is the linear system
\begin{equation}
(\mathbf{I}+\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi)\Z_k=\X_{k-1}+\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}\YH+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}\YM,
\label{eq:normal}
\end{equation}
and one gradient step from $\X_{k-1}$ is its linearization,
\begin{equation}
\Z_k\approx\X_{k-1}-\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\D_\theta\X_{k-1}-\YH)-\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\X_{k-1}-\YM).
\label{eq:linstep}
\end{equation}
An unfolded network replaces the proximal map by a learned module. The derivation already says two things about such a network: the ratio $\beta_k/\alpha_k=\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2/\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2$ should follow the noise of the two observations, and the operators enter only through $\D_\theta$, $\R_\phi$ and their adjoints, so they can be replaced at test time without retraining.

\subsection{Separable structure and the fusion null space}
\label{sec:kron}
A Gaussian blur aligned with the image axes is separable, and padding and decimation act on rows and columns independently. The operators therefore factor exactly, border included:
\begin{equation}
\D_\theta\X=\X\times_2\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\times_3\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}},\qquad \R_\phi\X=\X\times_1\mathbf{r}_\phi,
\label{eq:modes}
\end{equation}
where $\times_n$ is the mode-$n$ product, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times H}$ and $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{w\times W}$ pad, blur and decimate one axis, and $\mathbf{r}_\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times B}$ holds the spectral responses.
\begin{theorem}[Closed-form data step and null space]
\label{thm:kron}
Let $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{r}})\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\top}$, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{c}})\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top}$ and $\mathbf{r}_\phi^{\top}\mathbf{r}_\phi=\mathbf{V}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\nu})\mathbf{V}^{\top}$, and let $\mathcal{Q}(\cdot)=(\cdot)\times_1\mathbf{V}\times_2\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}\times_3\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}$.
(a) $\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta$ and $\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi$ are diagonal in the orthonormal basis $\mathcal{Q}$, with eigenvalues $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j$ and $\nu_b$.
(b) The solution of~\eqref{eq:normal} is
\begin{equation}
\Z_k=\mathcal{Q}\Big(\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{C}_k)\oslash\big[1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b\big]_{bij}\Big),
\label{eq:kronstep}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{C}_k$ the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:normal} and $\oslash$ element-wise division.
(c) The joint null space $\mathcal{N}(\D_\theta)\cap\mathcal{N}(\R_\phi)$ is spanned by the basis tensors with $\nu_b=0$ and $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j=0$. If $\mathbf{r}_\phi$, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}$ and $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}$ have full row rank, its dimension is $(B-M)(HW-hw)$, a fraction $(1-M/B)(1-1/s^2)$ of the unknowns.
\end{theorem}
The proof (\supp) uses the mixed-product rule: every factor of $\mathcal{Q}$ diagonalizes one mode. The step~\eqref{eq:kronstep} costs three mode products in each direction, $O(BHW(B+H+W))$ operations, and needs no iteration. It is exact for any separable kernel, not only a Gaussian, with any border rule and sub-pixel offset per axis, and for any linear spectral response, given eigendecompositions of an $H\times H$, a $W\times W$ and a $B\times B$ matrix, cached per image size; FUSE~\cite{wei2015fuse} needs periodic borders. A non-separable or spatially varying kernel breaks the Kronecker structure, and the network then takes a conjugate-gradient step with the same operator. Part (c) states what any method must infer from a prior: the high spatial frequencies of the spectral directions the multispectral sensor does not see, 88\% of the unknowns on Chikusei and 90\% on Pavia Centre at $\times4$ (all high frequencies, $1-1/s^2$ of the unknowns, without the HR-MSI). Because spatial detail is shared across bands, the $M$ observed directions carry \qone{msi-chikusei4-detail}\% of its energy on Chikusei and \qone{msi-pavia4-detail}\% on Pavia Centre: the minimum-norm image consistent with both observations reaches \qone{msi-chikusei4-psnr-both}~dB on Chikusei, against \qone{msi-chikusei4-psnr-lr-only}~dB from the LR-HSI alone. The split of every method's error in \cref{sec:nullsplit} rests on this null space.

\subsection{The cost of an inexact operator}
\label{sec:mismatch}
Stack the data terms as $\mathbf{A}=[\D_\theta;\R_\phi]$, $\mathbf{Y}=[\YH;\YM]$ and $\mathbf{W}_k=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_k\mathbf{I},\beta_k\mathbf{I})$, and let $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ be the operator a network actually uses: one with a zero border, a learned one, or one with wrong parameters. Write $T(\mathbf{V})=(\mathbf{I}+\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k\tilde{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}(\mathbf{V}+\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{Y})$ for the exact data step and $G(\mathbf{V})=\mathbf{V}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{Y})$ for its linearization.
\begin{theorem}[Fixed point and operator mismatch]
\label{thm:mismatch}
Let $\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{A}\X^*$. (a) If $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{A}$, then $T(\X^*)=G(\X^*)=\X^*$ for all $\alpha_k,\beta_k>0$. (b) For any $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, with $\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$,
\begin{equation}
\|T(\X^*)-\X^*\|\le\tfrac12\|\mathbf{e}\|,\qquad \|G(\X^*)-\X^*\|\le\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\|\,\|\mathbf{e}\|.
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
(c) If stage $k$ computes $\X_k=(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}_k)(T_k(\X_{k-1}))$ with a residual prior that satisfies $\mathcal{P}_k(\X^*)=\mathbf{0}$ and makes $\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}_k$ $L_k$-Lipschitz, then a $K$-stage network started at $\X^*$ returns $\|\X_K-\X^*\|\le\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big(\prod_{j=k}^{K}L_j\big)\|\mathbf{e}_k\|$, where $\mathbf{e}_k$ is $\mathbf{e}$ for the weights $\mathbf{W}_k$ of stage $k$.
\end{theorem}
The bounds follow from the singular values $b_i/(1+b_i^2)\le\tfrac12$ of $(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\top}$, $\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, and from the nonexpansiveness of $T_k$ (\supp). With the exact operator the ground truth is a fixed point of every data step; with an inexact one every data step moves it, by an image-dependent amount that no fixed correction removes. The exact step bounds this displacement whatever the learned weights are, the gradient step does not, and giving the network the true operator of other data restores (a), which a learned operator cannot do.
\begin{corollary}[Border rule]
\label{cor:border}
If $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ differs from $\mathbf{A}$ only in the border rule of $\D_\theta$, then $(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$ is nonzero only on the low-resolution pixels whose kernel support reaches the padding, i.e., within $\lceil p/s\rceil$ pixels of the border for a $(2p+1)\times(2p+1)$ kernel.
\end{corollary}
For our benchmarks only the first low-resolution row and column are affected: 3\% of a $64\times64$ test image, but 12\% of the $16\times16$ patches on which networks are trained.

\subsection{Estimating the operators}
\label{sec:ident}
\begin{theorem}[Identifiability and stability]
\label{thm:ident}
Let $\D_\theta$ act identically on every band and $\R_\phi$ identically on every pixel, with kernels and responses that sum to one, and let $\mathbf{r}(\theta,\phi)=\D_\theta\YM-\R_\phi\YH$. (a) $\D_\theta\R_{\phi'}=\R_{\phi'}\D_\theta$, so for noise-free data generated with $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$ the criterion
\begin{equation}
J(\theta,\phi)=\|\mathbf{r}(\theta,\phi)\|^2=\|\D_\theta\YM-\R_\phi\YH\|^2
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
vanishes at $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$. (b) $J(\theta,\phi)=0$ if and only if $\X$ lies in the null space of $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}=\D_\theta\R_{\phi^*}-\R_\phi\D_{\theta^*}$, and $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}\neq0$ whenever $(\D_\theta,\R_\phi)\neq(\D_{\theta^*},\R_{\phi^*})$: two configurations are confused only on a proper subspace of images, such as spatially constant images (blur and offsets) or images with equal bands (response shift). (c) If the Jacobian $\mathbf{G}$ of $\mathbf{r}$ with respect to $(\theta,\phi)$ has full column rank at $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$, this point is an isolated zero of $J$ over the continuous parameters, and with observation noise the minimizer of $J$ moves, to first order, by $-\mathbf{G}^{+}\mathbf{n}$ with $\mathbf{n}=\D_{\theta^*}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}}-\R_{\phi^*}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}}$, hence by at most $\|\mathbf{n}\|/\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}[From noise to reconstruction]
\label{cor:chain}
Under (c), the exact data step with the estimated operator moves the ground truth by at most $\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\mathbf{A}'\|\,\|\mathbf{n}\|/(2\,\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G}))$ to first order, with $\mathbf{A}'$ the Jacobian of $(\theta,\phi)\mapsto\mathbf{A}_{\theta\phi}\X^*$.
\end{corollary}
Unlike uniqueness on a finite grid, (b) is exact for continuous families, and (c) makes identifiability quantitative: $\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G})$, the conditioning of the estimation problem, is the stability constant, and the corollary carries observation noise through the operator estimate to the reconstruction (proofs in \supp). $\theta$ may collect two blur widths and two grid offsets. On the test images $\mathbf{G}$ has full rank for these five parameters (condition number \qone{opan-chikusei-condnorm} with unit-norm columns on Chikusei); at 30~dB SNR, (c) predicts errors of \qone{opan-chikusei-crb30-off}~px for the offsets and \qone{opan-chikusei-crb30-sigma}~px for the blur width, against \qone{opan-chikusei-rmse30-off} and \qone{opan-chikusei-rmse30-sigma}~px measured, the excess being a second-order noise bias (\supp). Part (a) is the relation HySure~\cite{simoes2015hysure} uses. We minimize $J$ on a grid (steps $0.025\,\sigma_0$ and 1~nm); since $\mathbf{r}$ is linear in the kernel, a blur of any shape can also be estimated by least squares (\cref{sec:cond}).

\section{ExactFuse}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Stages.} ExactFuse (\cref{fig:arch}) unrolls HQS for $K=3$ stages from the bicubic upsampling $\X_0$ of $\YH$. Stage $k$ first takes a data step with the exact operators: the learned spectral step~\eqref{eq:klstep} below, or, for non-separable operators and in the base model of \cref{sec:ablation}, the linearized step
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Z_k=\X_{k-1}&-s^2\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\D_\theta\X_{k-1}-\YH)\\
&-\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\X_{k-1}-\YM)
\end{split}
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
The step sizes $\alpha_k=\mathrm{softplus}(a_k)$ and $\beta_k=\mathrm{softplus}(b_k)$ are learned per stage, and the factor $s^2$ compensates the $1/s^2$ gain of $\D^{\top}\D$ on smooth images. The prior step is a learned residual, $\X_k=\Z_k+\Delta_k$ with $(\Delta_k,\mathbf{F}_k)=\mathcal{P}_k([\Z_k,\R_\phi^{+}\YM,s^2\D^{\top}(\D\Z_k-\YH),\tfrac{B}{M}\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\Z_k-\YM)],\mathbf{F}_{k-1})$. Here $\R_\phi^{+}\YM$ are the minimum-norm spectra in the $B$ hyperspectral bands that reproduce the HR-MSI ($\R_\phi\R_\phi^{+}=\mathbf{I}$, so they determine $\YM$ exactly), and the last two inputs tell the prior where the estimate still disagrees with each observation. Every input lives in the hyperspectral bands, so the prior does not depend on the number or the responses of the multispectral bands. $\mathbf{F}_{k-1}$ are the decoder features of the previous stage (cross-stage memory~\cite{song2021madun}). The last layer of every prior starts at zero, so training starts from plain HQS, for which the condition $\mathcal{P}_k(\X^*)=\mathbf{0}$ of \cref{thm:mismatch}(c) holds. All operator applications run in 32-bit precision.

\paragraph{Learned spectral data step.} In the basis of \cref{thm:kron} the exact step multiplies each component of the data residual by $1/(1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b)$, the filter of a white Gaussian prior. Images are not white and noise does not affect all components equally, so we let two small networks modulate these gains:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Z_k&=\X_{k-1}+\mathcal{Q}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{G}_k)\odot\bm{\Gamma}_k\big),\\
\Gamma_{k,bij}&=\frac{\exp\!\big(\ln2\,\tanh(u_k(\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j)+v_k(\nu_b))\big)}{1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b},
\end{split}
\label{eq:klstep}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{G}_k=\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\YH-\D_\theta\X_{k-1})+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\YM-\R_\phi\X_{k-1})$ and $u_k$, $v_k$ are two-layer perceptrons of the log-eigenvalues (and of the observation code below), initialized to zero so that training starts from~\eqref{eq:kronstep}. The gain factors over space and spectrum, so the perceptrons run $HW+B$ times per stage, not $BHW$ times.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:kl}
For any $u_k,v_k$: (a) with exact operators and noise-free data, $\X^*$ is a fixed point of~\eqref{eq:klstep}; (b) the step is nonexpansive; (c) with an inexact operator it moves $\X^*$ by at most $\|\mathbf{e}\|$, twice the bound~\eqref{eq:bound} of the exact step.
\end{proposition}
The gain lies in $(\tfrac12,2)$ times the exact one, which keeps every factor of the step in $(-1,1]$ (\supp). The learned step can thus change how much the network trusts each observed component, but it cannot move a correct estimate or amplify errors.

\paragraph{Prior.} Each prior is a three-level U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015unet} of Restormer blocks~\cite{zamir2022restormer} (\cref{fig:arch}b,c), whose transposed attention forms a channel-by-channel attention map with a cost linear in the number of pixels. ExactFuse holds \qone{puformer-params}~M parameters for Chikusei, almost all in the three priors. Their design is not our contribution, and \cref{sec:ablation} shows that a convolutional mixer works about as well.

\paragraph{Training over a family of observation models.} A network trained with one operator pair on noise-free data adapts its priors to that blur, that response and no noise. Since the operators enter ExactFuse only through its data steps, we can instead draw for every batch a blur width $\sigma\sim\mathcal{U}[0.5,2]\sigma_0$, a spectral shift $\delta\sim\mathcal{U}[-12,12]$~nm and, with probability 0.5, noise at an SNR from $\mathcal{U}[30,50]$~dB, simulate the batch with $(\D_\sigma,\R_\delta)$, and use the same operators in the data steps. At test time the operators are given or estimated with~\eqref{eq:J}. As HQS suggests, we also condition on the noise: a code $\mathbf{c}=(\sigma/\sigma_0-1,\delta/12,\log_{10}n_{\mathrm{H}}+2,\log_{10}n_{\mathrm{M}}+2)$, with the relative noise levels $n$ estimated from each observation by the median absolute deviation of its second differences, sets the step sizes $\alpha_k=\mathrm{softplus}(a_k+\mathbf{u}_k^{\top}\mathbf{c})$, $\beta_k=\mathrm{softplus}(b_k+\mathbf{v}_k^{\top}\mathbf{c})$ and modulates the prior features (FiLM~\cite{perez2018film}), similar to the hyper-parameter module of USRNet~\cite{zhang2020usrnet}.

\paragraph{Loss.} We train with $\|\X_K-\X\|_1+0.1\sum_{k<K}\|\X_k-\X\|_1+0.05\,\mathrm{SAM}(\X_K,\X)+0.1\,(1-\mathrm{SSIM}(\X_K,\X))$ and AdamW, and test the exponential moving average of the weights.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Data.} Chikusei~\cite{yokoya2016chikusei} has 128 bands (363--1018~nm) and is paired with the eight WorldView-2 responses; Pavia Centre~\cite{gamba2004pavia} has 92 bands and is paired with the four IKONOS responses. We use the split sizes of~\cite{chen2026twostage} (8 and 4 test images of $256\times256$ pixels) with fixed, disjoint locations given in \supp. CAVE~\cite{yasuma2010cave} (20 training and 12 test scenes) and Harvard~\cite{chakrabarti2011harvard} (30 and 20 images) have 31 bands and are paired with a Nikon RGB response. All pairs follow Wald's protocol~\cite{vivone2015critical}: both observations are simulated from the reference with~\eqref{eq:obs}, every training crop on its own. At $\times8$ and $\times16$ the blur is a $(2s-1)\times(2s-1)$ Gaussian with $\sigma=s/2$.

\paragraph{Baselines and budget.} We compare with GSA~\cite{aiazzi2007gsa} and \qone{n-retrained-word} networks: \qone{retrained-list}. Each is built from its authors' code; only the numbers of bands and the scale factor are changed (\supp\ lists the commits). All methods, ExactFuse included, are trained for 4~h on one Tesla T4 GPU with the same patches ($64\times64$), batch size (8), augmentation, EMA weights and validation-only model selection; SSR-NET, which converges sooner, gets 1.5~h. \subsection{Where the margin comes from}
\label{sec:nullsplit}

We reduce ExactFuse step by step (\supp). A single U-Net prior on the bicubic image and the HR-MSI, without data steps or stages, already reaches \qone{gain-chikusei-abl-unet-psnr}~dB on Chikusei and \qone{gain-pavia-abl-unet-psnr}~dB on Pavia Centre, above every retrained network. Most of the margin therefore comes from this residual multiscale prior and its training recipe, not from the transformer blocks, and the exact data steps add \qone{gain-chikusei-abl-unet-gap} and \qone{gain-pavia-abl-unet-gap}~dB on top. The recipe is not the whole story: with the same recipe for both, ExactFuse leads MIMFormer by \qone{recipe-chikusei-ours-gap} and \qone{recipe-pavia-ours-gap}~dB under ours and by \qone{recipe-chikusei-base-gap} and \qone{recipe-pavia-base-gap}~dB under the baselines'.

The null space of \cref{thm:kron}(c) gives a second view: we split the error of every method into its part in the observed subspace $\mathrm{row}(\D)+\mathrm{row}(\R)$, which contradicts the observations, and its null-space part, which only a prior can reduce. Bicubic interpolation and GSA err mostly in the observed subspace (only \qone{null-chikusei-bicubic-share}\% and \qone{null-chikusei-gsa-share}\% of their squared error lies in the null space on Chikusei). ExactFuse's observed error (column Obs.\ of \cref{tab:bench}) is \qone{null-chikusei-obs-ratio-best} times smaller than that of the best retrained network on Chikusei (\qone{null-chikusei-obs-best}) and \qone{null-pavia-obs-ratio-best} times on Pavia Centre. Removing MIMFormer's observed error alone would raise its PSNR by \qone{null-chikusei-consistency-gain}~dB on Chikusei; the remaining \qone{null-chikusei-prior-gain}~dB of ExactFuse's margin come from a better null-space estimate. The exact data steps account for the first part, the prior for the second.

\subsection{What the exact operator does}
\label{sec:ablation}
\qtab{cvpr_ablation}

\Cref{tab:ablation} retrains variants from scratch with the same budget. Its top block goes from the base model to ExactFuse. The closed-form step with learned gains and the HR-MSI residual input each raise the agreement with the LR-HSI (\qone{kcab-kl-consH} and \qone{kcab-mres-consH} against \qone{kcab-base-consH}~dB on Chikusei) at nearly equal PSNR; combined, with every input in the hyperspectral bands, they change the PSNR by \qone{kcab-pf-delta} and \qone{cab-pf-delta}~dB (Chikusei, Pavia Centre) and raise the agreement to \qone{kcab-pf-consH}~dB, with fewer iterations in the budget (\qone{kcab-pf-iters} against \qone{kcab-base-iters}). A learned gate that confines the prior to the null space lowers the PSNR (\qone{kcab-gate-vspf} and \qone{cab-gate-vspf}~dB), and so does, on Pavia Centre, a branch that transfers the HR-MSI's detail to all bands (\qone{cab-ntp-vspf}~dB); ExactFuse uses neither.\par Without data steps ExactFuse loses \qone{kcab-ng-nophys-absvspf} and \qone{cab-ng-nophys-absvspf}~dB and its agreement with the LR-HSI falls from \qone{kcab-pf-consH} to \qone{kcab-ng-nophys-consH}~dB; a gradient step instead of the closed form keeps the PSNR but lowers the agreement to \qone{kcab-ng-grad-consH}~dB. In the base model, a zero border or a learned blur and response cost \qone{kabl-zeros-absdelta} and \qone{kabl-learned-absdelta}~dB on Chikusei and reproduce the LR-HSI about 3~dB worse, as \cref{thm:mismatch} predicts; over three seeds the zero border costs \qone{kabl-seed-gain} and \qone{abl-seed-gain}~dB, against a seed spread of \qone{kabl-full-std} and \qone{abl-full-std}~dB.

The difference becomes large when the blur changes (\supp): given the true blur, the exact base model loses at most \qone{kabl-full-swaploss}~dB on Chikusei, the zero-border model \qone{kabl-zeros-swaploss}~dB, although its operator differs only at the image border, and the learned operator, which cannot be replaced, \qone{kabl-learned-fixedloss}~dB. A convolutional mixer instead of attention changes the PSNR by only \qone{kabl-conv-delta} and \qone{abl-conv-delta}~dB (Chikusei, Pavia Centre).

\subsection{One network for a family of observation models}
\label{sec:cond}

\Cref{tab:cond} tests networks trained over the operator family on conditions that leave the training protocol. Within the family the estimate of \cref{thm:ident} is accurate: over all Chikusei test images and conditions, 30~dB SNR included, its largest errors are \qone{cond-chikusei-sigerr-max}~px in blur width and \qone{cond-chikusei-shifterr-max}~nm in spectral shift. Training over the family makes the network robust to noise and combined changes: at 40--30~dB SNR the mean PSNR of ExactFuse rises from \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-bench-puformer-fixed} to \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-cond-ng-estimated}~dB on Chikusei, against \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-cond-mimformer-fixed}~dB for MIMFormer trained on the same randomized data. In the base model, the estimated operator adds to randomized training alone (e.g., \qone{cond-chikusei-combined-cond-omc-estimated} against \qone{cond-chikusei-combined-cond-nocond-fixed}~dB under combined changes), and MIMFormer stays below both. The price is a lower PSNR at the training operator (\qone{cond-chikusei-nominal-cond-ng-estimated} against \qone{chikusei4-psnr}~dB). One network trained on random responses also serves seven real sensors it never saw, \qone{fam-chikusei-vsmim-min}--\qone{fam-chikusei-vsmim-max}~dB above MIMFormer trained the same way on Chikusei and \qone{fam-chikusei-own-cost}~dB below ExactFuse trained for WorldView-2, and one network trained at $\times$4 and $\times$8 leads the best retrained network at the unseen $\times$16 by \qone{scl-chikusei-x16-gain}~dB (\supp).

Outside the family a parametric estimate fails: with an anisotropic or defocus blur ExactFuse drops to \qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-ng-estimated}~dB on Chikusei. Because $\mathbf{r}$ is linear in the kernel, a kernel of any shape can instead be estimated by least squares; with it ExactFuse reaches \qone{np-chikusei-ng-kernel} and \qone{np-pavia-ng-kernel}~dB under these blurs, as with the true kernel (\qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-ng-oracle} and \qone{cond-pavia-kernel-cond-ng-oracle}~dB) and ahead of MIMFormer trained on the same randomized data (\qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-mimformer-fixed} and \qone{cond-pavia-kernel-cond-mimformer-fixed}~dB). An HR-MSI misregistered by half a pixel costs every method, GSA included, more than 15~dB. Registering it before fusion with the offset estimated from the pair recovers most of this loss (\supp): for a sharp half-pixel shift, as a sensor records it, ExactFuse reaches \qone{reg-chikusei-puformer-reg_msi-sharp05}~dB on Chikusei (MIMFormer \qone{reg-chikusei-mimformer-reg_msi-sharp05}, GSA \qone{reg-chikusei-puformer-gsa_reg-sharp05}~dB), and with 35~dB noise added, trained over the wider family, \qone{reg-chikusei-regng-reg_msi-sharpnoise05}~dB (MIMFormer, trained without noise, \qone{reg-chikusei-mimformer-reg_msi-sharpnoise05}~dB). The linear estimate thus extends the operator family to any spatially invariant kernel.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~Results with a reference rest on simulated observations (Wald's protocol), as in every benchmark of this field; on real NEON data (spectrometer and RGB camera, \supp), ExactFuse is again the most accurate at reduced resolution (\qone{neon-puformer-psnr} against \qone{neon-mimformer-psnr}~dB for MIMFormer), and has the best no-reference score at full resolution (HQNR \qone{neon-puformer-hqnr} against \qone{neon-mimformer-hqnr}), but its operator variants differ by less than 0.1~dB there, because the fitted camera response is only approximate; the sensor tests use real responses on simulated pairs. (ii)~Exact operators help only when the operator family contains the true operator: spatially varying blurs fall outside even the least-squares kernel estimate, which costs \qone{np-chikusei-ng-snr30-cost}--\qone{np-pavia-ng-snr30-cost}~dB at 30~dB SNR, registration leaves several decibels of a misregistration loss (registration-aware networks such as IR\&ArF~\cite{qu2025irarf} were not retrained), non-separable kernels lose the closed form, and corrupted pixels in a four-band HR-MSI are tolerated but not localized (\supp). (iii)~All methods train for 4~h; the strongest baselines are close to converged (\supp), and 8~h narrows the gap but keeps it. (iv)~The Chikusei and Pavia Centre test sets are small; three seeds, bootstrap intervals (\supp), the pooled test and CAVE and Harvard back the comparison. (v)~ExactFuse takes \qone{pc-total-s}~s per $256\times256$ pair on a T4, operator estimate included, about twice as long as MIMFormer; its closed-form step is cheaper than a conjugate-gradient step (\qone{pc-kl-s} against \qone{pc-cg5-s}~s for the network), and the cached eigendecompositions take \qone{pc-size1024-eig-s}~s at $1024\times1024$ (\supp).

Exact separable operators with the true border give unfolded fusion a closed-form data step, an explicit null space, a bounded effect of operator errors and operators identifiable from the two observations. Under one protocol and budget, ExactFuse is more accurate than \qone{n-retrained-word} retrained networks, mainly through its prior and training, and its exact operators let one network adapt to new blurs, responses, noise, sensors and scale factors. Spatially varying operators, band-dependent noise and real paired references remain open. Code will be made publicly available.
