\title{ExactFuse: Closed-Form Unfolding with Exact Operators for Sensor-Independent Hyperspectral and Multispectral Image Fusion}

\author{\cvprauthors}   % hidden in review mode

\begin{abstract}
Hyperspectral cameras capture hundreds of bands at low spatial resolution, multispectral cameras a few bands at high resolution. Deep unfolding networks fuse the two well, but they learn or approximate the blur and spectral response instead of using the true operators, and they must be retrained for every sensor pair. We propose ExactFuse, an unfolding network built on exact operators. With a separable blur and the border rule of the simulation, the data step has a closed form, to which we add learned per-component gains, and the part of the image that neither observation constrains (88\% of the unknowns on Chikusei) becomes explicit. For almost every scene the true operators are identifiable from the two observations, so we estimate them at test time. An exact data step keeps the true image fixed, and a wrong operator displaces it by a bounded amount whatever the learned weights. The transformer prior sees the multispectral image only as spectra in the hyperspectral bands, and training covers a range of blurs, responses and noise levels, so one network serves different sensors. Against \qone{n-retrained-word} published networks retrained from their authors' code under a common budget, ExactFuse is the most accurate on Chikusei and Pavia Centre, by \qone{chikusei4-gain} and \qone{pavia4-gain}~dB, and in eight further settings up to $\times16$, and it reproduces the low-resolution input \qone{chikusei4-cons-gain}~dB more accurately than the strongest baseline. Much of the gain is due to the prior and its training; the exact operators matter most when the blur, sensor or noise changes. A half-pixel misregistration still costs every network trained on registered pairs, ExactFuse included, more than 15~dB, and registering the images beforehand recovers most but not all of it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Hyperspectral cameras measure hundreds of narrow bands at coarse spatial resolution, multispectral cameras a few broad bands at fine resolution, and fusing the two yields a high-resolution hyperspectral image (HR-HSI). Most recent methods are deep networks trained on simulated pairs: convolutional networks~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet}, transformers~\cite{li2024mimformer,deng2023psrt,ma2024reciprocal}, state-space models and diffusion models~\cite{chen2026twostage}.

Deep unfolding networks~\cite{huang2022dhif} unroll an iterative solver: each stage takes a data step that reduces the mismatch with the observations and then applies a learned prior. The data step needs the blur, the downsampling and the spectral response. Most unfolding networks learn these as convolutions or use zero padding, although the benchmark data are generated with a known blur and a different border rule, so even the ground truth fails the network's own consistency check. Each trained network is also tied to one sensor pair.

Evaluation is a further problem. Splits, responses, normalization and metric code vary between papers; PSRT~\cite{deng2023psrt}, for instance, is reported at 44.60~dB PSNR on Chikusei $\times$4 in one recent paper (\supp) and at 50.67~dB in~\cite{chen2026twostage}, so copied numbers cannot rank methods.

We study what exact operators contribute to an unfolded fusion network. We formulate fusion as maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation with parametric operators, unroll half-quadratic splitting (HQS)~\cite{zhang2020usrnet} and analyze the resulting data step. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A theory of exact operators in unfolded fusion that relates observation noise to reconstruction error. For a separable blur with the correct border rule, the data step has a closed form and the fusion null space is explicit (88\% of the unknowns on Chikusei). The operators are identifiable from the observation pair over continuous parameters, with an exact condition for when two sensor configurations are indistinguishable and a stability bound under noise, and an operator error moves the reconstruction by an amount bounded independently of the learned weights (\cref{sec:theory}). None of this holds for learned or zero-padded operators.
\item ExactFuse, an unfolded network with exact, replaceable operators and a learned spectral data step that keeps these guarantees. All its inputs lie in the hyperspectral bands, so one set of weights serves any multispectral sensor of known response; it is trained over a family of observation models and estimates the operators at test time (\cref{sec:method}). Its building blocks are standard and not claimed.
\item An equal-budget benchmark of \qone{n-retrained-word} published networks retrained from their authors' code, with a split of the error into an observed and a null-space part that shows where each method fails (\cref{sec:exp}). It also shows where ExactFuse's advantage comes from: mostly from the prior and its training, which stay ahead under a common recipe, while the exact operators make the output consistent with the inputs and help most when the operator changes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Deep fusion networks} predict the HR-HSI directly from the two observations without using the observation model. They are based on convolutions~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet}, transformers~\cite{li2024mimformer,deng2023psrt,ma2024reciprocal,fang2024mimosst,yang2026clsnet}, general fusion backbones, state-space models or diffusion~\cite{chen2026twostage}. SSA slices its convolution kernels according to the number of bands and reconstructs at arbitrary scales, which allows one model to handle several sensors, but it does not model their spectral responses.

\paragraph{Unfolding and operator knowledge.} Algorithm unrolling maps the iterations of a solver to the stages of a network. MHF-Net, MoG-DCN and DHIF-Net~\cite{huang2022dhif} unfold fusion solvers with learned or approximate operators, and later work improves the prior or the proximal update~\cite{yu2025otpnet}. IR\&ArF unfolds the fusion of unregistered images and learns the registration. In single-image super-resolution, USRNet~\cite{zhang2020usrnet} passes the blur kernel to a closed-form data step with periodic borders, and blind methods are trained on randomized degradations. We extend this idea to fusion, which involves two operators, and use the border rule of the simulation.

\paragraph{Closed forms and separable operators.} FUSE and a later method based on Hessian inversion (\supp) solve the quadratic fusion problem in closed form, assuming periodic borders. Tensor methods exploit separable degradations to prove that low-rank models can be recovered. Our closed form is valid for the reflect border used in the simulation and is implemented as a differentiable layer whose operators may differ for every image. Its learned filters are related to deep Wiener deconvolution, but they are bounded so that the step remains nonexpansive.

\paragraph{Range and null space.} Range--null-space networks split the reconstruction into a component determined by the measurements and a null-space component, and back-projection has been used to improve the consistency of pansharpening. With exact separable operators the corresponding projection has a closed form (\cref{thm:kron}), which we use to decompose the error of every method.

\section{Exact operators in unfolded fusion}
\label{sec:theory}
\subsection{Observation model and unfolding}
\label{sec:hqs}
Let $\X\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times H\times W}$ denote the HR-HSI. The two observations are modeled as
\begin{equation}
\YH=\D_\theta\X+\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}},\qquad \YM=\R_\phi\X+\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}},
\label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
where $\D_\theta$ applies reflect padding to each band, followed by a Gaussian blur of width $\theta$ and decimation by a factor $s$ starting at index zero, so that $\YH\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times h\times w}$ with $h=H/s$ and $w=W/s$. The operator $\R_\phi$ applies the $M$ spectral responses of the multispectral sensor to each pixel, shifted in wavelength by $\phi$~nm, and $\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}},\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}}$ are white Gaussian noise with standard deviations $\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}},\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}$. The benchmarks use $s=4$, a $7\times7$ blur with $\theta=\sigma_0=2$, $\phi=0$ and no noise~\cite{chen2026twostage}. We implement $\D_\theta$ together with its exact adjoint. In the adjoint, the transpose of reflect padding adds the padded margins back to the pixels from which they were copied (\supp). The adjoint test $\langle\D\X,\mathbf{Y}\rangle=\langle\X,\D^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\rangle$ is satisfied to within $10^{-13}$ in double precision.

Assuming a prior $p(\X)\propto\exp(-\lambda\Phi(\X))$, the MAP estimate minimizes $\tfrac{1}{2\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2}\|\D_\theta\X-\YH\|^2+\tfrac{1}{2\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2}\|\R_\phi\X-\YM\|^2+\lambda\Phi(\X)$. HQS introduces an auxiliary image $\Z$, coupled to $\X$ through the penalty $\tfrac{\mu_k}{2}\|\Z-\X\|^2$, and alternates between a data step and a prior step, $\X_k=\mathrm{prox}_{(\lambda/\mu_k)\Phi}(\Z_k)$. Setting $\alpha_k=1/(\mu_k\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2)$ and $\beta_k=1/(\mu_k\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2)$, the data step reduces to the linear system
\begin{equation}
(\mathbf{I}+\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi)\Z_k=\X_{k-1}+\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}\YH+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}\YM,
\label{eq:normal}
\end{equation}
and a single gradient step from $\X_{k-1}$ gives its linearization,
\begin{equation}
\Z_k\approx\X_{k-1}-\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\D_\theta\X_{k-1}-\YH)-\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\X_{k-1}-\YM).
\label{eq:linstep}
\end{equation}
In an unfolded network, the proximal map is replaced by a learned module. Two properties of such a network follow directly from the derivation. First, the ratio $\beta_k/\alpha_k=\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2/\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2$ should reflect the relative noise levels of the two observations. Second, the operators appear only through $\D_\theta$, $\R_\phi$ and their adjoints, and can therefore be exchanged at test time without retraining.

\subsection{Separable structure and the fusion null space}
\label{sec:kron}
A Gaussian blur aligned with the image axes is separable, and padding and decimation act independently on rows and columns. Consequently, the operators factor exactly, including at the border:
\begin{equation}
\D_\theta\X=\X\times_2\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\times_3\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}},\qquad \R_\phi\X=\X\times_1\mathbf{r}_\phi,
\label{eq:modes}
\end{equation}
where $\times_n$ denotes the mode-$n$ product, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times H}$ and $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{w\times W}$ perform padding, blurring and decimation along one axis, and $\mathbf{r}_\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times B}$ contains the spectral responses.
\begin{theorem}[Closed-form data step and null space]
\label{thm:kron}
Let $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{r}})\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\top}$, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{c}})\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top}$ and $\mathbf{r}_\phi^{\top}\mathbf{r}_\phi=\mathbf{V}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\nu})\mathbf{V}^{\top}$, and let $\mathcal{Q}(\cdot)=(\cdot)\times_1\mathbf{V}\times_2\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}\times_3\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}$.
(a) $\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta$ and $\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi$ are diagonal in the orthonormal basis $\mathcal{Q}$, with eigenvalues $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j$ and $\nu_b$.
(b) The solution of~\eqref{eq:normal} is
\begin{equation}
\Z_k=\mathcal{Q}\Big(\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{C}_k)\oslash\big[1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b\big]_{bij}\Big),
\label{eq:kronstep}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{C}_k$ the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:normal} and $\oslash$ element-wise division.
(c) The joint null space $\mathcal{N}(\D_\theta)\cap\mathcal{N}(\R_\phi)$ is spanned by the basis tensors with $\nu_b=0$ and $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j=0$. If $\mathbf{r}_\phi$, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}$ and $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}$ have full row rank, its dimension is $(B-M)(HW-hw)$, a fraction $(1-M/B)(1-1/s^2)$ of the unknowns.
\end{theorem}
The proof (\supp) uses the mixed-product rule: each factor of $\mathcal{Q}$ diagonalizes one mode. Step~\eqref{eq:kronstep} costs three mode products in each direction, $O(BHW(B+H+W))$ operations, without iteration. It holds for any separable kernel, not only a Gaussian, with any border rule and sub-pixel offset per axis, and for any linear spectral response, and needs only eigendecompositions of an $H\times H$, a $W\times W$ and a $B\times B$ matrix, cached per image size; FUSE needs periodic borders. A non-separable or spatially varying kernel breaks the Kronecker structure, and the network then takes a conjugate-gradient step with the same operator. Part (c) states what any method must infer from a prior: the high spatial frequencies of the spectral directions the multispectral sensor does not see, 88\% of the unknowns on Chikusei and 90\% on Pavia Centre at $\times4$ (without the HR-MSI, all high frequencies, $1-1/s^2$ of the unknowns). Because spatial detail is largely shared across bands, the $M$ observed directions carry \qone{msi-chikusei4-detail}\% of its energy on Chikusei and \qone{msi-pavia4-detail}\% on Pavia Centre, and the minimum-norm image consistent with both observations reaches \qone{msi-chikusei4-psnr-both}~dB on Chikusei, against \qone{msi-chikusei4-psnr-lr-only}~dB from the LR-HSI alone. The error split of \cref{sec:nullsplit} rests on this null space.

\subsection{The cost of an inexact operator}
\label{sec:mismatch}
We stack the data terms as $\mathbf{A}=[\D_\theta;\R_\phi]$, $\mathbf{Y}=[\YH;\YM]$ and $\mathbf{W}_k=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_k\mathbf{I},\beta_k\mathbf{I})$, and denote by $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ the operator that a network actually uses, which may have a zero border, be learned, or have incorrect parameters. We write $T(\mathbf{V})=(\mathbf{I}+\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k\tilde{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}(\mathbf{V}+\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{Y})$ for the exact data step and $G(\mathbf{V})=\mathbf{V}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{Y})$ for its linearization.
\begin{theorem}[Fixed point and operator mismatch]
\label{thm:mismatch}
Let $\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{A}\X^*$. (a) If $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{A}$, then $T(\X^*)=G(\X^*)=\X^*$ for all $\alpha_k,\beta_k>0$. (b) For any $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, with $\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$,
\begin{equation}
\|T(\X^*)-\X^*\|\le\tfrac12\|\mathbf{e}\|,\qquad \|G(\X^*)-\X^*\|\le\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\|\,\|\mathbf{e}\|.
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
(c) If stage $k$ computes $\X_k=(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}_k)(T_k(\X_{k-1}))$ with a residual prior that satisfies $\mathcal{P}_k(\X^*)=\mathbf{0}$ and makes $\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}_k$ $L_k$-Lipschitz, then a $K$-stage network started at $\X^*$ returns $\|\X_K-\X^*\|\le\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big(\prod_{j=k}^{K}L_j\big)\|\mathbf{e}_k\|$, where $\mathbf{e}_k$ is $\mathbf{e}$ for the weights $\mathbf{W}_k$ of stage $k$.
\end{theorem}
The bounds follow from the singular values $b_i/(1+b_i^2)\le\tfrac12$ of $(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\top}$, $\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, and the nonexpansiveness of $T_k$ (\supp). With the exact operator the ground truth is a fixed point of every data step; with an inexact one each data step moves it by an image-dependent amount that no fixed correction can remove. The exact step bounds this displacement whatever the learned weights, the gradient step does not, and supplying the true operator of new data restores (a), which a learned operator cannot do.
\begin{corollary}[Border rule]
\label{cor:border}
If $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ differs from $\mathbf{A}$ only in the border rule of $\D_\theta$, then $(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$ is nonzero only on the low-resolution pixels whose kernel support reaches the padding, i.e., within $\lceil p/s\rceil$ pixels of the border for a $(2p+1)\times(2p+1)$ kernel.
\end{corollary}
In our benchmarks, only the first low-resolution row and column are affected. This corresponds to 3\% of a $64\times64$ test image, but to 12\% of the $16\times16$ patches on which the networks are trained.

\subsection{Estimating the operators}
\label{sec:ident}
\begin{theorem}[Identifiability and stability]
\label{thm:ident}
Let $\D_\theta$ act identically on every band and $\R_\phi$ identically on every pixel, with kernels and responses that sum to one, and let $\mathbf{r}(\theta,\phi)=\D_\theta\YM-\R_\phi\YH$. (a) $\D_\theta\R_{\phi'}=\R_{\phi'}\D_\theta$, so for noise-free data generated with $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$ the criterion
\begin{equation}
J(\theta,\phi)=\|\mathbf{r}(\theta,\phi)\|^2=\|\D_\theta\YM-\R_\phi\YH\|^2
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
vanishes at $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$. (b) $J(\theta,\phi)=0$ if and only if $\X$ lies in the null space of $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}=\D_\theta\R_{\phi^*}-\R_\phi\D_{\theta^*}$, and $\mathcal{C}_{\theta\phi}\neq0$ whenever $(\D_\theta,\R_\phi)\neq(\D_{\theta^*},\R_{\phi^*})$: two configurations are confused only on a proper subspace of images, such as spatially constant images (blur and offsets) or images with equal bands (response shift). (c) If the Jacobian $\mathbf{G}$ of $\mathbf{r}$ with respect to $(\theta,\phi)$ has full column rank at $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$, this point is an isolated zero of $J$ over the continuous parameters, and with observation noise the minimizer of $J$ moves, to first order, by $-\mathbf{G}^{+}\mathbf{n}$ with $\mathbf{n}=\D_{\theta^*}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}}-\R_{\phi^*}\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}}$, hence by at most $\|\mathbf{n}\|/\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}[From noise to reconstruction]
\label{cor:chain}
Under (c), the exact data step with the estimated operator moves the ground truth by at most $\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\mathbf{A}'\|\,\|\mathbf{n}\|/(2\,\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G}))$ to first order, with $\mathbf{A}'$ the Jacobian of $(\theta,\phi)\mapsto\mathbf{A}_{\theta\phi}\X^*$.
\end{corollary}
Unlike uniqueness on a finite grid, (b) is exact for continuous families, and (c) makes identifiability quantitative: the stability constant is $\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{G})$, the conditioning of the estimation problem, and the corollary carries observation noise through the operator estimate to the reconstruction (proofs in \supp). $\theta$ may hold two blur widths and two grid offsets. On the test images $\mathbf{G}$ has full rank for these five parameters (condition number \qone{opan-chikusei-condnorm} with unit-norm columns on Chikusei); at 30~dB SNR, (c) predicts errors of \qone{opan-chikusei-crb30-off}~px for the offsets and \qone{opan-chikusei-crb30-sigma}~px for the blur width, and we measure \qone{opan-chikusei-rmse30-off} and \qone{opan-chikusei-rmse30-sigma}~px, the excess being a second-order noise bias (\supp). HySure uses the relation of part (a). We minimize $J$ on a grid (steps $0.025\,\sigma_0$ and 1~nm); since $\mathbf{r}$ is linear in the kernel, a blur of any shape can also be estimated by least squares (\cref{sec:cond}).

\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Stages.} ExactFuse (\cref{fig:arch}) unrolls $K=3$ stages of HQS, starting from the bicubic upsampling $\X_0$ of $\YH$. Each stage $k$ begins with a data step that uses the exact operators. This is either the learned spectral step~\eqref{eq:klstep} described below or, for non-separable operators and in the base model of \cref{sec:ablation}, the linearized step
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Z_k=\X_{k-1}&-s^2\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\D_\theta\X_{k-1}-\YH)\\
&-\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\X_{k-1}-\YM)
\end{split}
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
The step sizes $\alpha_k=\mathrm{softplus}(a_k)$ and $\beta_k=\mathrm{softplus}(b_k)$ are learned for each stage, and the factor $s^2$ compensates for the $1/s^2$ gain of $\D^{\top}\D$ on smooth images. The prior step adds a learned residual, $\X_k=\Z_k+\Delta_k$ with $(\Delta_k,\mathbf{F}_k)=\mathcal{P}_k([\Z_k,\R_\phi^{+}\YM,s^2\D^{\top}(\D\Z_k-\YH),\tfrac{B}{M}\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\Z_k-\YM)],\mathbf{F}_{k-1})$. Here $\R_\phi^{+}\YM$ are the minimum-norm spectra in the $B$ hyperspectral bands that reproduce the HR-MSI ($\R_\phi\R_\phi^{+}=\mathbf{I}$, so they determine $\YM$ exactly), and the last two inputs show the prior where the estimate still disagrees with each observation. As every input lies in the hyperspectral bands, the prior does not depend on the number or responses of the multispectral bands. $\mathbf{F}_{k-1}$ are the decoder features of the previous stage (cross-stage memory). The last layer of each prior starts at zero, so training starts from plain HQS, which satisfies $\mathcal{P}_k(\X^*)=\mathbf{0}$ in \cref{thm:mismatch}(c). Operators run in 32-bit precision.

\paragraph{Learned spectral data step.} In the basis of \cref{thm:kron}, the exact step scales each component of the data residual by $1/(1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b)$, which is the filter corresponding to a white Gaussian prior. Natural images are not white, and noise does not affect all components equally. We therefore use two small networks to modulate these gains:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Z_k&=\X_{k-1}+\mathcal{Q}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{G}_k)\odot\bm{\Gamma}_k\big),\\
\Gamma_{k,bij}&=\frac{\exp\!\big(\ln2\,\tanh(u_k(\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j)+v_k(\nu_b))\big)}{1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b},
\end{split}
\label{eq:klstep}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{G}_k=\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\YH-\D_\theta\X_{k-1})+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\YM-\R_\phi\X_{k-1})$, and $u_k$, $v_k$ are two-layer perceptrons that take the log-eigenvalues (and the observation code introduced below) as input. They are initialized to zero, so that training starts from~\eqref{eq:kronstep}. Since the gain factorizes over space and spectrum, the perceptrons are evaluated $HW+B$ times per stage rather than $BHW$ times.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:kl}
For any $u_k,v_k$: (a) with exact operators and noise-free data, $\X^*$ is a fixed point of~\eqref{eq:klstep}; (b) the step is nonexpansive; (c) with an inexact operator it moves $\X^*$ by at most $\|\mathbf{e}\|$, twice the bound~\eqref{eq:bound} of the exact step.
\end{proposition}
The gain lies between $\tfrac12$ and $2$ times the exact gain, which keeps every factor of the step in $(-1,1]$ (\supp). The learned step can therefore adjust how much the network relies on each observed component, but it can neither move a correct estimate nor amplify errors.

\paragraph{Prior.} Each prior is a three-level U-Net built from Restormer blocks~\cite{zamir2022restormer} (\cref{fig:arch}b,c). Their transposed attention computes an attention map across channels, at a cost that grows linearly with the number of pixels. ExactFuse has \qone{puformer-params}~M parameters for Chikusei, almost all of which belong to the three priors. We do not consider the prior architecture a contribution, and \cref{sec:ablation} shows that a convolutional mixer performs about equally well.

\paragraph{Training over a family of observation models.} A network trained with one operator pair on noise-free data adapts its priors to that blur, that response and no noise. Since the operators enter ExactFuse only through its data steps, we instead draw for every batch a blur width $\sigma\sim\mathcal{U}[0.5,2]\sigma_0$, a spectral shift $\delta\sim\mathcal{U}[-12,12]$~nm and, with probability 0.5, noise at an SNR from $\mathcal{U}[30,50]$~dB, simulate the batch with $(\D_\sigma,\R_\delta)$ and use the same operators in the data steps. At test time the operators are given or estimated with~\eqref{eq:J}. As HQS suggests, we also condition on the noise: a code $\mathbf{c}=(\sigma/\sigma_0-1,\delta/12,\log_{10}n_{\mathrm{H}}+2,\log_{10}n_{\mathrm{M}}+2)$, in which the relative noise level $n$ of each observation is estimated as the median absolute deviation of its second differences, sets the step sizes $\alpha_k=\mathrm{softplus}(a_k+\mathbf{u}_k^{\top}\mathbf{c})$ and $\beta_k=\mathrm{softplus}(b_k+\mathbf{v}_k^{\top}\mathbf{c})$ and modulates the prior features through FiLM, much like the hyper-parameter module of USRNet~\cite{zhang2020usrnet}.

\paragraph{Loss.} The training loss is $\|\X_K-\X\|_1+0.1\sum_{k<K}\|\X_k-\X\|_1+0.05\,\mathrm{SAM}(\X_K,\X)+0.1\,(1-\mathrm{SSIM}(\X_K,\X))$. We optimize it with AdamW and evaluate the exponential moving average of the weights.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Data.} Chikusei~\cite{yokoya2016chikusei} has 128 bands (363--1018~nm) and is paired with the eight WorldView-2 responses, Pavia Centre~\cite{gamba2004pavia} has 92 bands and is paired with the four IKONOS responses. We use the split sizes of~\cite{chen2026twostage} (8 and 4 test images of $256\times256$ pixels) at fixed, disjoint locations listed in \supp. CAVE (20 training, 12 test scenes) and Harvard (30 and 20 images) have 31 bands and are paired with a Nikon RGB response. All pairs follow Wald's protocol: both observations are simulated from the reference with~\eqref{eq:obs}, each training crop separately. At $\times8$ and $\times16$, the blur is a $(2s-1)\times(2s-1)$ Gaussian with $\sigma=s/2$.

\paragraph{Baselines and budget.} We compare with GSA~\cite{aiazzi2007gsa} and \qone{n-retrained-word} networks: \qone{retrained-list} (further references in \supp). Each is built from its authors' code with only the numbers of bands and the scale factor changed (commits in \supp). All methods, ExactFuse included, are trained for 4~h on one Tesla T4 GPU with the same $64\times64$ patches, batch size (8), augmentation, EMA weights and validation-only model selection; SSR-NET, which converges sooner, gets 1.5~h. The published networks keep their learning rates, with an L1 loss. One code computes all metrics: PSNR (peak equal to the dataset maximum), SSIM, the spectral angle SAM (degrees) and ERGAS; Q2n, CC, SCC and RMSE are in \supp.

\subsection{Where the margin comes from}
\label{sec:nullsplit}

We remove the components of ExactFuse one at a time (\supp). A single U-Net prior on the bicubic image and the HR-MSI, with no data steps and one stage, already reaches \qone{gain-chikusei-abl-unet-psnr}~dB on Chikusei and \qone{gain-pavia-abl-unet-psnr}~dB on Pavia Centre, above every retrained network. Most of the margin thus comes from this residual multiscale prior and its training recipe rather than from the transformer blocks, and the exact data steps add \qone{gain-chikusei-abl-unet-gap} and \qone{gain-pavia-abl-unet-gap}~dB. The recipe does not explain everything: trained with the same recipe, ExactFuse leads MIMFormer by \qone{recipe-chikusei-ours-gap} and \qone{recipe-pavia-ours-gap}~dB with ours and by \qone{recipe-chikusei-base-gap} and \qone{recipe-pavia-base-gap}~dB with the baselines'.

The null space of \cref{thm:kron}(c) gives a second view. We split each method's error into a part in the observed subspace $\mathrm{row}(\D)+\mathrm{row}(\R)$, which contradicts the observations, and a null-space part, which only a prior can reduce. Bicubic interpolation and GSA err mostly in the observed subspace (on Chikusei only \qone{null-chikusei-bicubic-share}\% and \qone{null-chikusei-gsa-share}\% of their squared error is in the null space). ExactFuse's observed error (column Obs.\ of \cref{tab:bench}) is \qone{null-chikusei-obs-ratio-best} times smaller than that of the best retrained network on Chikusei (\qone{null-chikusei-obs-best}) and \qone{null-pavia-obs-ratio-best} times smaller on Pavia Centre. Removing only MIMFormer's observed error would raise its PSNR by \qone{null-chikusei-consistency-gain}~dB on Chikusei; the other \qone{null-chikusei-prior-gain}~dB of ExactFuse's margin come from a better null-space estimate. We attribute the first part to the exact data steps and the second to the prior.

\subsection{What the exact operator does}
\label{sec:ablation}
\qtab{cvpr_ablation}

All variants in \cref{tab:ablation} are retrained from scratch under the same budget; the top block builds ExactFuse from the base model. The closed-form step with learned gains and the HR-MSI residual input each improve the consistency with the LR-HSI (\qone{kcab-kl-consH} and \qone{kcab-mres-consH} against \qone{kcab-base-consH}~dB on Chikusei) at almost the same PSNR. Combined, with all inputs in the hyperspectral bands, they change the PSNR by \qone{kcab-pf-delta} and \qone{cab-pf-delta}~dB (Chikusei, Pavia Centre) and raise the consistency to \qone{kcab-pf-consH}~dB. A learned gate that confines the prior to the null space lowers the PSNR (\qone{kcab-gate-vspf} and \qone{cab-gate-vspf}~dB), so ExactFuse does not use it.\par Without data steps ExactFuse loses \qone{kcab-ng-nophys-absvspf} and \qone{cab-ng-nophys-absvspf}~dB, and its consistency with the LR-HSI falls from \qone{kcab-pf-consH} to \qone{kcab-ng-nophys-consH}~dB; a gradient step in place of the closed form keeps the PSNR but lowers the consistency to \qone{kcab-ng-grad-consH}~dB. The learned gains, the residual input and $\R^{+}\YM$ each change the PSNR by at most \qone{ab-small-max}~dB; one stage, a convolutional mixer or no cross-stage memory by at most \qone{ab-arch-max}~dB; a zero border by \qone{kcab-ng-zeros-absvspf} and \qone{cab-ng-zeros-absvspf}~dB\qoneif{cab-ng-learned-psnr}{; and learned operators by \qone{kcab-ng-learned-absvspf} and \qone{cab-ng-learned-absvspf}~dB}. In the base model, a zero border or a learned blur and response cost \qone{kabl-zeros-absdelta} and \qone{kabl-learned-absdelta}~dB on Chikusei and reproduce the LR-HSI about 3~dB worse, as \cref{thm:mismatch} predicts.

The differences grow when the blur changes (\supp). Given the true blur, the exact base model loses at most \qone{kabl-full-swaploss}~dB on Chikusei, the zero-border model \qone{kabl-zeros-swaploss}~dB, and the model with a learned operator, which cannot be replaced, \qone{kabl-learned-fixedloss}~dB.

\subsection{One network for a family of observation models}
\label{sec:cond}

\Cref{tab:cond} tests networks trained over the operator family on conditions outside the training protocol. Within the family the estimate of \cref{thm:ident} is accurate: over all Chikusei test images and conditions, 30~dB SNR included, its largest errors are \qone{cond-chikusei-sigerr-max}~px in blur width and \qone{cond-chikusei-shifterr-max}~nm in spectral shift. Training over the family makes the network robust to noise and combined changes. At 40--30~dB SNR the mean PSNR of ExactFuse on Chikusei rises from \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-bench-puformer-fixed} to \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-cond-ng-estimated}~dB, against \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-cond-mimformer-fixed}~dB for MIMFormer trained on the same randomized data, at the cost of a lower PSNR under the training operator (\qone{cond-chikusei-nominal-cond-ng-estimated} against \qone{chikusei4-psnr}~dB). One network trained on random responses also handles seven real sensors it never saw: on Chikusei it is \qone{fam-chikusei-vsmim-min}--\qone{fam-chikusei-vsmim-max}~dB above MIMFormer trained the same way and \qone{fam-chikusei-own-cost}~dB below ExactFuse trained for WorldView-2. A network trained at $\times$4 and $\times$8 leads the best retrained network at the unseen $\times$16 by \qone{scl-chikusei-x16-gain}~dB (\supp).

Outside the family the parametric estimate fails: with an anisotropic or defocus blur ExactFuse drops to \qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-ng-estimated}~dB on Chikusei. Because $\mathbf{r}$ is linear in the kernel, a kernel of any shape can instead be estimated by least squares. ExactFuse then reaches \qone{np-chikusei-ng-kernel} and \qone{np-pavia-ng-kernel}~dB under these blurs, as with the true kernel (\qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-ng-oracle} and \qone{cond-pavia-kernel-cond-ng-oracle}~dB) and ahead of MIMFormer trained on the same randomized data (\qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-mimformer-fixed} and \qone{cond-pavia-kernel-cond-mimformer-fixed}~dB). A half-pixel misregistration of the HR-MSI costs GSA and every network trained on registered pairs more than 15~dB. Registering the HR-MSI first, with the offset estimated from the pair, recovers most of this (\supp): for a sharp half-pixel shift, as a sensor would record it, ExactFuse reaches \qone{reg-chikusei-puformer-reg_msi-sharp05}~dB on Chikusei (MIMFormer \qone{reg-chikusei-mimformer-reg_msi-sharp05}~dB, GSA \qone{reg-chikusei-puformer-gsa_reg-sharp05}~dB).

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~Results with a reference rely on simulated observations (Wald's protocol). On real NEON data (\supp) ExactFuse is again the most accurate at reduced resolution (\qone{neon-puformer-psnr} against \qone{neon-mimformer-psnr}~dB for MIMFormer)\qoneif{sjer-gain}{ and leads by \qone{sjer-gain}~dB on an unseen site}, but its operator variants differ by less than 0.1~dB there, since the fitted camera response is only approximate; the sensor tests use real responses on simulated pairs. (ii)~Exact operators help only when the operator family contains the true operator. Spatially varying blurs fall outside even the least-squares kernel estimate, which costs \qone{np-chikusei-ng-snr30-cost}--\qone{np-pavia-ng-snr30-cost}~dB at 30~dB SNR, and registration still leaves a misregistration loss of several decibels\qoneif{reg-chikusei-m2dtnf-msi_frame-sharp05}{ (the registration-aware M2DTN~\cite{dong2024m2dtn}, retrained on misregistered pairs and applied without registration, outperforms it for bilinear shifts on Pavia Centre, \qone{reg-pavia-m2dtnf-msi_frame-pure} vs.\ \qone{reg-pavia-puformer-reg_msi-pure}~dB, \supp)}\qoneelse{reg-chikusei-m2dtnf-msi_frame-sharp05}{ (registration-aware networks such as IR\&ArF were not retrained)}. Non-separable kernels lose the closed form, and corrupted pixels in a four-band HR-MSI are tolerated but localized only by a model-free detector (\supp). (iii)~The Chikusei and Pavia Centre test sets are small; three seeds, bootstrap intervals (\supp), the pooled test and CAVE and Harvard support the comparison. (iv)~ExactFuse needs \qone{pc-total-s}~s per $256\times256$ pair on a T4, about twice as long as MIMFormer (\supp).

With exact separable operators and the correct border rule, unfolded fusion gets a closed-form data step, an explicit null space, bounded errors under operator mismatch and identifiable operators. ExactFuse outperforms \qone{n-retrained-word} retrained networks, mainly through its prior and training, and adapts to new operators, sensors and scale factors. Spatially varying operators and real paired references remain open. Code will be made publicly available.
