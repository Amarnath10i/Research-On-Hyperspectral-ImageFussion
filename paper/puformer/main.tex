\begin{abstract}
Deep unfolding networks for hyperspectral and multispectral image fusion alternate data-consistency steps with learned priors, but their observation operators are learned or approximated, and they are trained for one sensor pair. We formulate fusion as maximum a posteriori estimation with parametric blur and spectral-response operators, unroll its half-quadratic splitting, and establish three properties that make the operators usable inside the network. First, the two operators commute, and on any finite set of candidate parameters the true pair is identified by the two observations alone for almost every scene. Second, with a separable blur and the true border rule, the operators factor along the three axes of the image cube: the data step then has a closed form in a product eigenbasis, and the joint null space, the part of the image that no observation constrains, is explicit; it holds 88\% of the unknowns for Chikusei. Third, an exact data step leaves the ground truth fixed, and under an inexact operator it moves it by an amount bounded independently of the learned weights. PUFormer builds on these results: a closed-form data step whose per-component gains are learned without losing these guarantees, a residual transformer prior, and training over a family of blurs, responses and noise levels with the operators estimated at test time. Against five published networks retrained from their authors' code under one budget, PUFormer is the most accurate on Chikusei and Pavia Centre, by \qone{chikusei4-gain} and \qone{pavia4-gain}~dB PSNR; its error in the observed subspace is \qone{null-pavia-obs-ratio}--\qone{null-chikusei-obs-ratio} times smaller, and the null-space split attributes its margin to consistency and to the prior. Trained over operator families, it keeps its accuracy under changed blurs, responses and noise; outside the family and under misregistration it fails, as the other methods do. Code, logs and all results are public.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{H}{yperspectral} imagers record hundreds of narrow spectral bands but, for a fixed photon budget, at a coarse spatial resolution; multispectral imagers do the opposite. Hyperspectral and multispectral image fusion recovers a high-resolution hyperspectral image (HR-HSI) from one observation of each kind~\cite{yokoya2017review,loncan2015review,vivone2025deep}. Classical methods formulate fusion as a regularized inverse problem, using component substitution~\cite{aiazzi2007gsa}, coupled matrix factorization~\cite{yokoya2012cnmf}, subspace regularization~\cite{simoes2015hysure} or fast Sylvester-equation solvers~\cite{wei2015fuse}. Deep networks have since become the dominant approach, from convolutional reconstruction networks~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet,ran2023guidednet} to transformers~\cite{deng2023psrt,fang2024mimosst,he2024lgct,fu2025ssdt} and diffusion models~\cite{cao2024ddif,chen2026twostage,xu2026cdps,zhu2025argsdiff}.

Model-guided and unfolded networks~\cite{xie2022mhfnet,dong2021mogdcn,huang2022dhif,fang2024dunet,tian2024hmpnet,sun2025iatun} bring the observation model back into the network: each stage takes a step that reduces the mismatch between the current estimate and the two observations, and a learned module then refines the estimate. How faithfully the network reproduces the observation model has received less attention than the design of the learned module. In most of these networks the blur--decimation and spectral operators are learned convolution layers, or are implemented with the zero padding that convolution layers apply at the image border. The benchmark data, however, are simulated with a fixed blur and a different border rule. The network's operator is then not the one that produced the data, and even the ground truth fails the network's own consistency check. In simulated benchmarks this mismatch is avoidable, because the forward model is known exactly. A second difficulty concerns evaluation: reported numbers on the same dataset vary widely across papers because of differences in sensor response, blur, data split, normalization and even metric definitions~\cite{vivone2025deep}, so improvements are only meaningful within one fully specified protocol in which all methods are run.

This paper treats the observation model inside an unfolded network as an object to be analyzed, estimated and exploited rather than approximated. We write fusion as maximum a posteriori estimation with parametric operators, unroll its half-quadratic splitting, and derive the properties that the operators of such a network have; the network, PUFormer, is built from them. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Theory of the observation operators in unfolded fusion.} We prove that the blur--decimation and spectral operators commute and that their parameters are identified by the two observations alone for almost every scene (Theorem~\ref{thm:ident}); that with a separable blur and the true border rule they factor along the three axes of the image cube, which gives the data step in closed form and the joint null space explicitly (Theorem~\ref{thm:kron}); and that an exact data step leaves the ground truth fixed, while an inexact operator moves it by an amount that the exact proximal step bounds independently of the learned weights, with a corollary for border rules (Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch}).
\item \textbf{An unfolded network built on these results.} PUFormer uses the closed-form exact data step, a learned spectral data step that reweights its eigencomponents while provably keeping the fixed point and nonexpansiveness (Proposition~\ref{prop:kl}), observation-model conditioning of the step sizes and the prior, and training over a family of blurs, spectral responses and noise levels with the operators estimated at test time.
\item \textbf{An evaluation that explains the results.} Five published networks are retrained from their authors' code under one budget and also with their own recipes; the null-space split of the error shows how much of each method's error contradicts the observations and attributes PUFormer's margin; ablations, tests inside and outside the operator family, and further datasets and scale factors delimit what the exact operators do and do not achieve.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Model-based fusion}
Component substitution methods such as GSA~\cite{aiazzi2007gsa} inject multispectral detail into the upsampled hyperspectral bands. Unmixing-based methods such as CNMF~\cite{yokoya2012cnmf} factor both observations with shared endmembers, and subspace methods such as HySure~\cite{simoes2015hysure} regularize the HR-HSI within a low-dimensional spectral subspace, whose dimension can be estimated with HySime~\cite{bioucas2008hysime}. FUSE~\cite{wei2015fuse} solves the resulting quadratic problem in closed form through a Sylvester equation. These methods use the forward model explicitly but rely on hand-crafted priors.

\subsection{Supervised deep fusion}
SSR-NET~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet} and GuidedNet~\cite{ran2023guidednet} learn a direct mapping from the two observations. Transformers model long-range spatial and spectral dependencies: PSRT~\cite{deng2023psrt} uses pyramid shuffle-and-reshuffle attention, MIMO-SST~\cite{fang2024mimosst} multi-input multi-output supervision, LGCT~\cite{he2024lgct} local--global collaboration, DSPNet~\cite{shan2023dspnet} a dual spatial--spectral pyramid, SSDT~\cite{fu2025ssdt} dilated attention, and MIMFormer~\cite{li2024mimformer}, AMSF-Net~\cite{liu2025amsf}, ASMNet~\cite{liu2025asmnet} and DPFormer~\cite{liu2026dpformer} multiscale spectral--spatial designs. Recent work also studies state-space models~\cite{peng2024fusionmamba,huang2026hdgmamba}, mixture-of-experts routing~\cite{xiao2026ramoe} and modality interaction~\cite{huo2026mian}. General image-fusion frameworks such as U2Net~\cite{peng2023u2net} and OTPNet~\cite{yu2025otpnet} have also been applied to this task.

\subsection{Model-guided and unfolded networks}
Algorithm unrolling~\cite{gregor2010lista,monga2021unrolling,zhang2018istanet} turns the iterations of an optimization method into network stages. MHF-Net~\cite{xie2022mhfnet}, MoG-DCN~\cite{dong2021mogdcn} and DHIF-Net~\cite{huang2022dhif} unfold fusion-specific solvers, MHF-Net and DHIF-Net with operators learned as convolution layers, DUNET~\cite{fang2024dunet} adds transformer priors for unregistered inputs, HMPNet~\cite{tian2024hmpnet} extends unfolding to three modalities, and an information-aware transformer-based unfolding network (ITU-Net)~\cite{sun2025iatun}, a mutual-guidance unfolding network (SMGU)~\cite{yan2025smgu}, the perceptive spectral transformer unfolding network (PSTUN)~\cite{wang2025pstun}, which also handles arbitrary scales, and the cross-domain-aware unfolding transformer CaFormer~\cite{cao2026caformer} strengthen the learned proximal steps with transformer priors. PUFormer belongs to this family, and none of its prior's components is new: passing features from one stage to the next follows memory-augmented unfolding~\cite{song2021madun}, spectral-wise attention inside an unfolded network appears in DAUHST~\cite{cai2022dauhst}, and the transposed attention is that of Restormer~\cite{zamir2022restormer}. A single unfolded network that takes the blur kernel as an input, and therefore handles kernels it was not trained on, was shown for single-image super-resolution by USRNet~\cite{zhang2020usrnet}.

\subsection{Boundary conditions}
In classical deconvolution, an image border handled differently by the model and by the data causes ringing that spreads inward, and reflexive (Neumann) boundary conditions, i.e., reflect padding, are a standard remedy~\cite{ng1999neumann,reeves2005boundary,hansen2006deblurring}. In iterative reconstruction, a back-projection that is not the exact adjoint of the forward model changes the point to which the iteration converges~\cite{zeng2000unmatched,savanier2021adjoint}, and in convolutional networks zero padding is itself a source of spatial bias near the border~\cite{alsallakh2021pad}. In unfolded fusion networks the operators are either learned or implemented with the zero padding of convolution layers, while benchmark data are simulated with other border rules; to our knowledge, the effect of this mismatch has not been examined. Our contribution is on this side: an unfolded network whose operators, border included, are exactly those of the simulation, and an ablation that separates the exact operator from a zero-border and a learned operator under the same budget.

\subsection{Generative fusion}
Diffusion models have been conditioned on the two observations (DDIF~\cite{cao2024ddif}), constrained by the observation model during sampling (CDPS~\cite{xu2026cdps}), restricted to a learned subspace (ARGS-Diff~\cite{zhu2025argsdiff}) or combined with differential attention in a two-stage conditioning scheme~\cite{chen2026twostage}. Null-space formulations~\cite{wang2023ddnm} make the data-consistency part of the reconstruction exact. The two-stage conditional diffusion model of Chen~\textit{et al.}~\cite{chen2026twostage} holds the best published Chikusei $\times$4 results in our survey and is our main reference; its comparison also includes the correlation-aware network CLSNet~\cite{yang2026clsnet} and the dual cross transformer (DCTransformer)~\cite{ma2024reciprocal}, denoted DCT in their table.

\section{Problem Formulation and Analysis}
\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Observation model}
Let $\X\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times H\times W}$ be the HR-HSI with $B$ bands ($B=128$ for Chikusei and 92 for Pavia Centre). The two observations are
\begin{equation}
\YH = \D_\theta\X+\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}},\qquad \YM = \R_\phi\X+\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}},
\label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
where $\D_\theta=\mathcal{S}\,\mathcal{K}_\theta\,\mathcal{P}$ applies, band by band, reflect padding $\mathcal{P}$ of width $p$, a valid correlation $\mathcal{K}_\theta$ with a normalized $(2p+1)\times(2p+1)$ Gaussian kernel of standard deviation $\theta$, and decimation $\mathcal{S}$ that keeps every $s$-th pixel starting at index zero, so $\YH\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times H/s\times W/s}$; $\R_\phi$ applies to every pixel the $M$ spectral responses of the multispectral sensor, shifted along wavelength by $\phi$~nm; and $\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{H}}$, $\mathbf{N}_{\mathrm{M}}$ are independent white Gaussian noise of standard deviation $\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}$ and $\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}$. The benchmark protocol is the special case $\theta=\sigma_0$, $\phi=0$ without noise, with $s=4$, $p=3$ and $\sigma_0=2$, the $7\times7$ blur of~\cite{chen2026twostage}; we write $\D=\D_{\sigma_0}$ and $\R=\R_0$ for the nominal operators and keep $s$ and $p$ general where the analysis does not depend on them. The spectral operator $\R\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times B}$ integrates the spectrum with the $M=8$ WorldView-2 band responses~\cite{digitalglobe2014srf} for Chikusei and with the $M=4$ IKONOS band responses~\cite{dial2003ikonos} for Pavia Centre. Because the manufacturers publish the responses only as plots together with band edges, we model each band as a flat top with logistic flanks (4~nm roll-off) that pass exactly through the published edges, at 5\% response for WorldView-2, whose edges are tabulated at that level, and at 50\% for the IKONOS band limits (blue 445--516, green 506--595, red 632--698 and near infrared 757--853~nm), and normalize every band to unit sum.

\textbf{Exact adjoint.} Every gradient step on $\|\D\X-\YH\|^2$ requires $\D^{\top}=\mathcal{P}^{\top}\mathcal{K}^{\top}\mathcal{S}^{\top}$. $\mathcal{S}^{\top}$ inserts zeros and $\mathcal{K}^{\top}$ is a full convolution; together they are a strided transposed convolution with an output padding of $s-1$. The adjoint of reflect padding folds the padded margins back onto the pixels they were copied from. For a padded row index $j\in[0,H+2p)$ the source row is $p-j$ for $j<p$, $j-p$ in the interior and $2H-2+p-j$ in the bottom margin, hence
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
(\mathcal{P}^{\top}\mathbf{g})_i={}&\mathbf{g}_{i+p}+[1\le i\le p]\,\mathbf{g}_{p-i}\\
&+[H-1-p\le i\le H-2]\,\mathbf{g}_{2H-2+p-i},
\end{split}
\label{eq:fold}
\end{equation}
applied to rows and then to columns. With~\eqref{eq:fold}, $\D$ in the network is exactly the simulation operator, and the inner-product test $\langle\D\X,\mathbf{Y}\rangle=\langle\X,\D^{\top}\mathbf{Y}\rangle$ holds to a relative error below $10^{-13}$ in double precision at every scale factor. $\R^{\top}$ is the transpose of the response matrix.

\subsection{MAP estimation and its unfolding}
\label{sec:hqs}
For given operator parameters and noise levels, and a prior $p(\X)\propto\exp(-\lambda\Phi(\X))$, the maximum a posteriori estimate is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\X}=\arg\min_{\X}\ \tfrac{1}{2\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2}\|\D_\theta\X-\YH\|^2+\tfrac{1}{2\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2}\|\R_\phi\X-\YM\|^2+\lambda\Phi(\X).
\label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
Half-quadratic splitting~\cite{zhang2020usrnet} introduces an auxiliary image $\Z$ coupled to $\X$ by a penalty $\tfrac{\mu_k}{2}\|\Z-\X\|^2$ and alternates, for $k=1,\dots,K$, a data step and a prior step:
\begin{align}
\Z_k&=\arg\min_{\Z}\ \tfrac{1}{2\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2}\|\D_\theta\Z-\YH\|^2+\tfrac{1}{2\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2}\|\R_\phi\Z-\YM\|^2\nonumber\\
&\qquad+\tfrac{\mu_k}{2}\|\Z-\X_{k-1}\|^2,\label{eq:xstep}\\
\X_k&=\mathrm{prox}_{(\lambda/\mu_k)\Phi}(\Z_k).\label{eq:zstep}
\end{align}
With $\alpha_k=1/(\mu_k\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2)$ and $\beta_k=1/(\mu_k\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2)$, the data step~\eqref{eq:xstep} is the symmetric positive definite linear system
\begin{equation}
(\mathbf{I}+\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi)\,\Z_k=\X_{k-1}+\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}\YH+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}\YM,
\label{eq:normal}
\end{equation}
which conjugate gradients solve with the exact operators and adjoints alone, and a single gradient step from $\X_{k-1}$ gives its linearization
\begin{equation}
\Z_k\approx\X_{k-1}-\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\D_\theta\X_{k-1}-\YH)-\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\X_{k-1}-\YM).
\label{eq:linstep}
\end{equation}
An unfolded network replaces the proximal map~\eqref{eq:zstep} by a learned module. Three properties of this derivation shape the network of Section~\ref{sec:method}. First, $\alpha_k$ and $\beta_k$ are not independent design parameters: their ratio $\beta_k/\alpha_k=\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2/\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2$ weights the two observations by their noise. Second, the proximal map depends on $\lambda/\mu_k$ and hence on the noise level, so a learned prior should be told it. Third, the operators enter only through $\D_\theta$, $\R_\phi$ and their adjoints in~\eqref{eq:normal}, so operators known or estimated at test time can be used without retraining. Theorems~\ref{thm:ident} and~\ref{thm:mismatch} state when the operators can be identified from the observations and what an inexact operator costs.

\subsection{Identifiability of the operators}
\label{sec:ident}
\begin{theorem}[Commutation and identifiability]
\label{thm:ident}
Let $\D_\theta$ apply the same padding, blur and decimation to every band and $\R_\phi$ the same matrix $\mathbf{r}_\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times B}$ to every pixel. (a) $\D_\theta\R_{\phi'}=\R_{\phi'}\D_\theta$ for all $\theta,\phi'$, with $\D_\theta$ acting on $M$ or $B$ bands. Hence, for noise-free observations generated with $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$, the criterion
\begin{equation}
J(\theta,\phi)=\|\D_\theta\YM-\R_\phi\YH\|^2
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
vanishes at $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$. (b) Let $\mathcal{G}$ be a finite set of candidate parameters on which $\theta\mapsto\D_\theta$ and $\phi\mapsto\mathbf{r}_\phi$ are injective, and let no column of $\mathbf{r}_{\phi}$ vanish. Then, for almost every $\X$ (with respect to the Lebesgue measure), $(\theta^*,\phi^*)$ is the unique zero of $J$ on $\mathcal{G}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{IEEEproof}
(a) On vectorized images, $\D_\theta=\mathbf{I}_B\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta$ and $\R_\phi=\mathbf{r}_\phi\otimes\mathbf{I}_N$, with $\bar{\D}_\theta$ the single-band spatial operator and $N=HW$; by the mixed-product rule, $(\mathbf{I}_M\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta)(\mathbf{r}_\phi\otimes\mathbf{I}_N)=\mathbf{r}_\phi\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta=(\mathbf{r}_\phi\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N/s^2})(\mathbf{I}_B\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta)$. With $\YM=\R_{\phi^*}\X$ and $\YH=\D_{\theta^*}\X$, $\D_{\theta^*}\YM=\R_{\phi^*}\D_{\theta^*}\X=\R_{\phi^*}\YH$.
(b) By (a), $J(\theta,\phi)=\|\mathbf{E}_{\theta\phi}\X\|^2$ with $\mathbf{E}_{\theta\phi}=\mathbf{r}_{\phi^*}\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta-\mathbf{r}_\phi\otimes\bar{\D}_{\theta^*}$. Suppose $\mathbf{E}_{\theta\phi}=\mathbf{0}$ and apply it to $\X=\mathbf{e}_b\otimes\mathbf{u}$, a single band $b$ with spatial pattern $\mathbf{u}$: $(\mathbf{r}_{\phi^*})_{:b}\otimes\bar{\D}_\theta\mathbf{u}=(\mathbf{r}_\phi)_{:b}\otimes\bar{\D}_{\theta^*}\mathbf{u}$ for all $\mathbf{u}$. If $\theta\neq\theta^*$, then $\bar{\D}_\theta\neq c\,\bar{\D}_{\theta^*}$ for every scalar $c$: both kernels sum to one and reflect padding preserves constants, so both operators map a constant image to the same constant, which forces $c=1$ and contradicts injectivity. Two linear maps of rank greater than one that are not scalar multiples of each other map some $\mathbf{u}$ to linearly independent images, and for this $\mathbf{u}$ the identity above forces $(\mathbf{r}_{\phi^*})_{:b}=(\mathbf{r}_\phi)_{:b}=\mathbf{0}$, a contradiction. If $\theta=\theta^*$, the identity gives $(\mathbf{r}_{\phi^*})_{:b}=(\mathbf{r}_\phi)_{:b}$ for every $b$, so $\phi=\phi^*$. Hence $\mathbf{E}_{\theta\phi}\neq\mathbf{0}$ for every $(\theta,\phi)\neq(\theta^*,\phi^*)$ in $\mathcal{G}$; its null space is a proper subspace and has measure zero, and so does the finite union of these null spaces. For every $\X$ outside it, $J(\theta,\phi)>0$ whenever $(\theta,\phi)\neq(\theta^*,\phi^*)$.
\end{IEEEproof}
We therefore estimate $(\theta,\phi)$ for each test pair by minimizing~\eqref{eq:J} on a grid (steps of $0.025\,\sigma_0$ and 1~nm), using only the two observations; HySure~\cite{simoes2015hysure} uses the same relation to estimate the responses of a model-based method. With noise, $\mathbb{E}\,J(\theta,\phi)=\|\mathbf{E}_{\theta\phi}\X\|^2+\tfrac{N}{s^2}\big(M\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2\|\mathbf{k}_\theta\|^2+\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2\|\mathbf{r}_\phi\|_F^2\big)$ up to border terms, where $\mathbf{k}_\theta$ is the blur kernel. The noise term decreases with $\theta$ and barely depends on $\phi$, so noise biases $\hat\theta$ upward by an amount that vanishes as the SNR grows. Theorem~\ref{thm:ident} also requires the family to contain the true operator; Section~\ref{sec:cond} tests anisotropic and defocus blurs and misregistered observations, which it does not.

\subsection{Separable structure: a closed-form data step and the fusion null space}
\label{sec:kron}
A Gaussian kernel, isotropic or aligned with the image axes, is separable, and reflect and zero padding treat rows and columns independently; decimation does too. The blur--decimation operator therefore factors exactly, border included: for the cube $\X\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times H\times W}$,
\begin{equation}
\D_\theta\X=\X\times_2\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\times_3\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}},\qquad \R_\phi\X=\X\times_1\mathbf{r}_\phi,
\label{eq:modes}
\end{equation}
where $\times_n$ is the mode-$n$ product and $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times H}$ and $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{w\times W}$ pad, blur and decimate a single axis. Separable operators of this form are used by tensor-factorization fusion methods~\cite{kanatsoulis2018stereo} to identify low-rank models, under periodic borders; here they yield an exact, closed-form data step and an explicit description of what fusion cannot observe.
\begin{theorem}[Diagonalization and null space]
\label{thm:kron}
Let $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathrm{r}})\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\top}$, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathrm{c}})\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top}$ and $\mathbf{r}_\phi^{\top}\mathbf{r}_\phi=\mathbf{V}\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\mathbf{V}^{\top}$ be eigendecompositions, and $\mathcal{Q}(\cdot)=(\cdot)\times_1\mathbf{V}\times_2\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{r}}\times_3\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{c}}$. (a) $\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta$ and $\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi$ are diagonal in the orthonormal basis $\mathcal{Q}$, with eigenvalues $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j$ for every band and $\nu_b$ for every pixel. (b) The exact data step~\eqref{eq:normal} is
\begin{equation}
\Z_k=\mathcal{Q}\Big(\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{C}_k)\oslash\big[1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b\big]_{b,i,j}\Big),
\label{eq:kronstep}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{C}_k$ the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:normal} and $\oslash$ the element-wise division: three mode products in each direction, $O(BHW(B+H+W))$ operations, no iteration and no periodic-border assumption. (c) The null space of the joint operator, $\mathcal{N}(\D_\theta)\cap\mathcal{N}(\R_\phi)$, is spanned by the basis tensors with $\nu_b=0$ and $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j=0$; its orthogonal projector is $(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{s}})\otimes(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{r}}\otimes\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{c}})$ in mode order, with $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{s}}$, $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{r}}$, $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{c}}$ the projectors onto the row spaces of $\mathbf{r}_\phi$, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}$, $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}$. If these have full row rank, its dimension is $(B-M)(HW-hw)$, a fraction $(1-M/B)(1-1/s^2)$ of all unknowns.
\end{theorem}
\begin{IEEEproof}
(a) By~\eqref{eq:modes}, $\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta$ applies $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}$ and $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}$ to the two spatial modes and the identity to the spectral mode, and $\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi$ applies $\mathbf{r}_\phi^{\top}\mathbf{r}_\phi$ to the spectral mode alone; each factor is diagonalized by the corresponding factor of $\mathcal{Q}$, and an identity factor by any orthonormal basis. (b) In this basis the matrix of~\eqref{eq:normal} is diagonal with entries $1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b>0$. (c) $\mathcal{N}(\D_\theta)\cap\mathcal{N}(\R_\phi)=\mathcal{N}(\D_\theta^{\top}\D_\theta+\R_\phi^{\top}\R_\phi)$, whose eigenvalues $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\nu_b$ are sums of nonnegative terms and vanish only when both terms do. The basis tensors with $\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j>0$ span the row space of $\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\otimes\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}$, of dimension $hw$, and those with $\nu_b>0$ the row space of $\mathbf{r}_\phi$, of dimension $M$, which gives the projector and the dimension.
\end{IEEEproof}
The closest closed form, that of FUSE~\cite{wei2015fuse}, solves the same system by Fourier transforms under periodic borders; \eqref{eq:kronstep} holds for the border rule of the simulation, which Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch} shows to matter, and is a differentiable layer whose operators may change from one image to the next, at the cost of eigendecompositions of an $H\times H$, a $W\times W$ and a $B\times B$ matrix. Part~(c) states what any fusion method has to infer from a prior: the high spatial frequencies of the spectral directions that the multispectral sensor does not see, 88\% of the unknowns for Chikusei and 90\% for Pavia Centre at $\times4$; every other component is determined by the observations. Section~\ref{sec:nullsplit} uses this to split the error of every method into a part that contradicts the observations and a part in this null space.

\subsection{The cost of an inexact operator}
\label{sec:mismatch}
Stack the two data terms as $\mathbf{A}=[\D_\theta;\R_\phi]$, $\mathbf{Y}=[\YH;\YM]$ and $\mathbf{W}_k=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_k\mathbf{I},\beta_k\mathbf{I})$, and let $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ be the operator a network uses in place of $\mathbf{A}$: one with a zero border, a learned one, or one with wrong parameters. Write $T(\mathbf{V})=(\mathbf{I}+\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k\tilde{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}(\mathbf{V}+\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{Y})$ for the exact data step~\eqref{eq:normal} applied to an estimate $\mathbf{V}$, and $G(\mathbf{V})=\mathbf{V}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{Y})$ for its linearization~\eqref{eq:linstep}.
\begin{theorem}[Fixed point and operator mismatch]
\label{thm:mismatch}
Let $\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{A}\X^*$. (a) If $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{A}$, then $T(\X^*)=G(\X^*)=\X^*$ for all $\alpha_k,\beta_k>0$. (b) For every $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$,
\begin{align}
\|T(\X^*)-\X^*\|&\le\tfrac12\,\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*\|,\label{eq:bexact}\\
\|G(\X^*)-\X^*\|&\le\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\|\,\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*\|.\label{eq:bgrad}
\end{align}
(c) Let stage $k$ of a network be $\X_k=(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}_k)(T_k(\X_{k-1}))$, where the exact data step $T_k$ uses $\mathbf{W}_k$ and the residual prior satisfies $\mathcal{P}_k(\X^*)=\mathbf{0}$ and makes $\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}_k$ $L_k$-Lipschitz. Started at $\X_0=\X^*$, the network returns
\begin{equation}
\|\X_K-\X^*\|\le\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Big(\prod_{j=k}^{K}L_j\Big)\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*\|.
\label{eq:bnet}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{IEEEproof}
(a) $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\X^*-\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{0}$ makes both updates vanish. (b) With $\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, a direct computation gives $T(\X^*)-\X^*=(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$; in the singular value decomposition of $\mathbf{B}$, $(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\top}$ has singular values $b_i/(1+b_i^2)\le\tfrac12$. Similarly $G(\X^*)-\X^*=\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$, whose norm is at most $\|\mathbf{B}\|$ times the last factor. (c) $T_k$ is nonexpansive, since $\|T_k(\mathbf{V})-T_k(\mathbf{V}')\|=\|(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{B})^{-1}(\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{V}')\|\le\|\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{V}'\|$. With $e_k=\|\X_k-\X^*\|$ and $(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{P}_k)(\X^*)=\X^*$, $e_k\le L_k\|T_k(\X_{k-1})-\X^*\|\le L_k\big(\|T_k(\X_{k-1})-T_k(\X^*)\|+\|T_k(\X^*)-\X^*\|\big)\le L_k(e_{k-1}+b_k)$, where $b_k$ is the bound~\eqref{eq:bexact}; unrolling from $e_0=0$ gives~\eqref{eq:bnet}.
\end{IEEEproof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch} separates the two roles of the operator. With the exact operator, the ground truth is a fixed point of every data step, so the priors only have to remove the reconstruction error. With an inexact one, every data step moves the ground truth, and the priors must also undo this displacement; because it depends on the image, it cannot be removed by a fixed correction. The exact proximal step bounds the displacement by $\tfrac12\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*\|$ whatever the learned weights, whereas for the gradient step the bound grows with $\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\|$, which training may make large, and the step is not nonexpansive, so~\eqref{eq:bnet} has no counterpart for it. The theorem also explains why an exact operator can be replaced at test time: for data generated by $\mathbf{A}'$, giving the network $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{A}'$ restores~(a), which a learned operator cannot do.
\begin{corollary}[Border rule]
\label{cor:border}
If $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ differs from $\mathbf{A}$ only in the border rule of $\D_\theta$, e.g., zero instead of reflect padding, $(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$ is nonzero only on the LR pixels whose $(2p+1)\times(2p+1)$ kernel support reaches the padding, i.e., within $\lceil p/s\rceil$ pixels of the LR border.
\end{corollary}
With decimation from index zero and $p<s$, only the first LR row and column are affected, a fraction $(h+w-1)/(hw)$ of an $h\times w$ LR image: 3\% of a $64\times64$ image but 12\% of the $16\times16$ LR patches on which networks are trained. The displacement~\eqref{eq:bexact} is then small, which matches the small accuracy gap at the training blur (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}), whereas a learned operator differs from $\mathbf{A}$ everywhere. In real images the true border rule is unknown~\cite{almeida2013boundaries}, and Corollary~\ref{cor:border} bounds what any fixed choice can cost; the main role of exactness is the one stated by Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch}: an operator that can be supplied, estimated and replaced. Section~\ref{sec:cond} measures the displacement and the test PSNR as functions of the operator residual $\|(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}-\mathbf{A})\X^*\|$.

\subsection{Unfolded iterations}
PUFormer (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}) is the HQS iteration of Section~\ref{sec:hqs} unrolled for $K=3$ stages, starting from the bicubic upsampling~\cite{keys1981cubic} $\X_0$ of $\YH$. Stage $k$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}, bottom) first performs the data step~\eqref{eq:xstep} with the exact operators, either linearized,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Z_k=\X_{k-1}&-s^2\alpha_k\,\D_\theta^{\top}(\D_\theta\X_{k-1}-\YH)\\
&-\beta_k\,\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\X_{k-1}-\YM),
\end{split}
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
or exactly, in closed form by~\eqref{eq:kronstep} (Fig.~\ref{fig:dstep}; by conjugate gradients on~\eqref{eq:normal} if the kernel is not separable). The weights $\alpha_k=\mathrm{softplus}(a_k)$ and $\beta_k=\mathrm{softplus}(b_k)$ stand for $1/(\mu_k\varsigma_{\mathrm{H}}^2)$ and $1/(\mu_k\varsigma_{\mathrm{M}}^2)$ in~\eqref{eq:normal} and are learned per stage; the factor $s^2$ compensates the $1/s^2$ gain of $\D^{\top}\D$ on smooth content under $\times s$ decimation, so that both terms start at a comparable scale. The prior step~\eqref{eq:zstep} is a learned residual,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
(\Delta_k,\mathbf{F}_k)&=\mathcal{P}_k\big([\Z_k,\YM,s^2\D^{\top}(\D\Z_k-\YH)],\,\mathbf{F}_{k-1}\big),\\
\X_k&=\Z_k+\Delta_k,
\end{split}
\label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
where the third input, the gradient of the LR-HSI data term at $\Z_k$, tells the prior where the updated estimate still disagrees with $\YH$, and $\mathbf{F}_{k-1}$ are the final decoder features of the previous stage (cross-stage memory, merged by a $1\times1$ convolution). A variant also gives the prior the back-projected HR-MSI residual $(B/M)\,\R_\phi^{\top}(\R_\phi\Z_k-\YM)$. The last convolution of every prior is initialized to zero, so training starts from the pure HQS iteration with $\mathcal{P}_k=\mathbf{0}$, which leaves the ground truth unchanged as required by Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch}(c). The image path, including all operator applications, stays in 32-bit floating point; only the priors run in 16-bit mixed precision~\cite{micikevicius2018mixed}.

\textbf{Learned spectral data step.} In the basis of Theorem~\ref{thm:kron}, the exact step multiplies every component of the data residual by the gain $1/(1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b)$, the filter of a white Gaussian prior. Natural images are not white, and noise does not harm all components equally, so we let two small networks modulate these gains:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Z_k&=\X_{k-1}+\mathcal{Q}\big(\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{G}_k)\odot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_k\big),\\
\Gamma_{k,bij}&=\frac{\exp\!\big(\ln2\,\tanh(u_k(\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j,\mathbf{c})+v_k(\nu_b,\mathbf{c}))\big)}{1+\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b},
\end{split}
\label{eq:klstep}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{G}_k=\alpha_k\D_\theta^{\top}(\YH-\D_\theta\X_{k-1})+\beta_k\R_\phi^{\top}(\YM-\R_\phi\X_{k-1})$ is the data residual and $u_k$, $v_k$ are two-layer perceptrons of the log-eigenvalue and of the observation code $\mathbf{c}$ introduced below, initialized to zero so that training starts from the exact step~\eqref{eq:kronstep}. The gain is separable, so a stage evaluates the networks $HW+B$ times rather than $BHW$ times.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:kl}
For all $u_k$, $v_k$: (a) with exact operators and noise-free data, $\X^*$ is a fixed point of~\eqref{eq:klstep}; (b) \eqref{eq:klstep} is nonexpansive; (c) with an inexact operator $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, it moves $\X^*$ by at most $\|\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*\|$, twice the bound~\eqref{eq:bexact} and independent of the weights.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Let $\varsigma_{bij}=\alpha_k\lambda^{\mathrm{r}}_i\lambda^{\mathrm{c}}_j+\beta_k\nu_b$, the eigenvalues of $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\mathbf{W}_k\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$, and $e^{f}$ the numerator of $\Gamma$, so $\tfrac12<e^{f}<2$. (a) $\mathbf{G}_k=\mathbf{0}$ at $\X^*$. (b) The step maps a difference $\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{V}'$ to $\mathcal{Q}\big((1-\Gamma\varsigma)\odot\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{V}')\big)$, and $0\le\Gamma\varsigma=e^{f}\varsigma/(1+\varsigma)<2$, so every factor lies in $(-1,1]$. (c) The displacement is $\mathcal{Q}\big(\Gamma\odot\mathcal{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{e})\big)$ with $\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ and $\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{W}_k^{1/2}(\mathbf{A}-\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\X^*$; its operator norm is $\max e^{f}\sqrt{\varsigma}/(1+\varsigma)\le2\cdot\tfrac12=1$.
\end{IEEEproof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch}(a) and~(c) therefore hold for the learned step too: it can reweight how much the network trusts each observed component, but it cannot move a correct estimate or amplify errors.

\subsection{Transposed-attention prior}
Each prior (Fig.~\ref{fig:prior}) is a three-level U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015unet} with widths 48, 96 and 192, two, three and four transformer blocks on the encoder levels and bottleneck, mirrored decoder levels, and two refinement blocks. A block (Fig.~\ref{fig:block}(a)) follows Restormer~\cite{zamir2022restormer}: multi-Dconv-head transposed attention (Fig.~\ref{fig:mdta}) computes queries, keys and values with $1\times1$ and depth-wise $3\times3$ convolutions, normalizes each feature map over space, and forms a $c'\times c'$ attention matrix across channels per head (1, 2 and 4 heads on the three levels) with a learned temperature. Attention is thus computed between spectral feature maps rather than between spatial positions, its cost is linear in the number of pixels, and the receptive field of every block covers the whole image. A gated depth-wise feed-forward network with expansion 2.66 follows (Fig.~\ref{fig:block}(b)). The three priors hold 3.37--3.38~M parameters each; the whole network has 10.13~M parameters for Chikusei and \pav{params}~M for Pavia Centre, whose input and output convolutions are smaller.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{PUFormer inference (unrolled HQS)}
\label{alg:puformer}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\YH$, $\YM$; operator parameters $(\theta,\phi)$, given or $\arg\min_{\mathcal{G}}J$ of~\eqref{eq:J}
\State build the exact $\D_\theta,\D_\theta^{\top},\R_\phi,\R_\phi^{\top}$ (reflect border,~\eqref{eq:fold})
\State $\X_0\gets\mathrm{bicubic}_{\times s}(\YH)$, $\mathbf{F}_0\gets\varnothing$
\For{$k=1,\dots,K$}
  \State $\Z_k\gets$ data step: \eqref{eq:step}, or CG on~\eqref{eq:normal} \Comment{fp32}
  \State $\mathbf{r}_k\gets s^2\,\D^{\top}(\D\Z_k-\YH)$
  \State $(\Delta_k,\mathbf{F}_k)\gets\mathcal{P}_k([\Z_k,\YM,\mathbf{r}_k],\mathbf{F}_{k-1})$ \Comment{fp16}
  \State $\X_k\gets\Z_k+\Delta_k$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\X_K$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Training objective}
With ground truth $\X$, the loss is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}={}&\|\X_K-\X\|_1+0.1\sum_{k<K}\|\X_k-\X\|_1\\
&+\lambda_{\mathrm{SAM}}\,\mathrm{SAM}(\X_K,\X)+\lambda_{\mathrm{S}}\big(1-\mathrm{SSIM}(\X_K,\X)\big),
\end{split}
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with mean absolute errors, deep supervision~\cite{lee2015dsn} of the intermediate stages, the mean spectral angle in radians, and a band-wise Gaussian SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim} whose data range is the maximum of each training patch. We use $\lambda_{\mathrm{SAM}}=0.05$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{S}}=0.1$; both terms are computed in 32-bit precision. An exponential moving average of the weights (decay 0.999)~\cite{polyak1992averaging} is used for validation and testing.

\subsection{Training for a family of observation models}
\label{sec:randtrain}
Trained with one fixed operator pair on noise-free data, the priors adapt to one blur, one spectral response and no noise, and Section~\ref{sec:robust} shows the consequences: a shifted spectral response or noise is not compensated by giving the network the true operator. Because the operators enter PUFormer only through its data steps, the same network can instead be trained for a family of observation models. For every batch we draw a blur width $\sigma\sim\mathcal{U}[0.5,2]\,\sigma_0$, a shift $\delta\sim\mathcal{U}[-12,12]$~nm of the spectral responses and, with probability 0.5, Gaussian noise on both observations at an SNR drawn from $\mathcal{U}[30,50]$~dB; the batch is simulated with $(\D_\sigma,\R_\delta)$, and the same operators are used in the data steps. At test time the operators are given, or estimated from the test pair by minimizing~\eqref{eq:J}. Training on randomized degradations is a standard remedy in blind super-resolution~\cite{zhang2018srmd,zhang2021bsrgan}, and USRNet~\cite{zhang2020usrnet} passes the blur kernel to the data step of an unfolded single-image super-resolution network. We extend this to the two operators of fusion, with exact borders and with both operators estimated from the observation pair, and compare it with randomized training alone, in which the data steps keep the nominal operator.

\textbf{Observation-model conditioning.} The data steps then know the operator, but the prior does not, and neither knows the noise level, although the HQS derivation (Section~\ref{sec:hqs}) shows that the weights $\alpha_k,\beta_k$ should follow the noise of the two observations and that the proximal step depends on it. We therefore describe each input pair by a code $\mathbf{c}=(\sigma/\sigma_0-1,\ \delta/12,\ \log_{10}n_{\mathrm{H}}+2,\ \log_{10}n_{\mathrm{M}}+2)$, where $(\sigma,\delta)$ are the parameters of the operator used in the data steps and $n$ is the noise standard deviation of an observation relative to its RMS, estimated from the observation itself as the median absolute diagonal second difference over all bands~\cite{donoho1994ideal}. The step sizes become $\alpha_k=\mathrm{softplus}(a_k+\mathbf{u}_k^{\top}\mathbf{c})$ and $\beta_k=\mathrm{softplus}(b_k+\mathbf{v}_k^{\top}\mathbf{c})$, so that the two data terms can be weighted by their noise as in a maximum a posteriori estimate, and an embedding of $\mathbf{c}$ (two linear layers) scales and shifts the features of every prior (FiLM~\cite{perez2018film}) after its input convolution, at the input of its bottleneck and before its output convolution. All added parameters start at zero, so training starts from the unconditioned network; at the nominal operator without noise, $\mathbf{c}$ is nearly constant and the network reduces to PUFormer.

\subsection{Self-ensemble}
At test time we average the prediction for the input with the transposed prediction for the transposed input~\cite{lim2017edsr}. Transposition preserves the decimation phase, so both passes see observations generated by exactly the same operator. Flips and rotations do not: flipping a 256-pixel row maps sampled position $4m$ to $255-4m$, which has phase three, so we do not use them.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\subsection{Data and protocol}
Chikusei~\cite{yokoya2016chikusei} is an airborne scene of $2517\times2335$ pixels with 128 bands from 363 to 1018~nm. The split sizes follow~\cite{chen2026twostage}: eight $256\times256$ test images, 64 validation patches and 832 training patches of $64\times64$. Because their locations are not specified there, we fix them as follows. We take the central $2048\times2048$ crop, which contains no no-data border, and divide it by its global maximum (15{,}133 digital numbers, DN). The test images are the eight $256\times256$ tiles of the top 256 rows, the validation patches tile rows 272--400, and training draws random $64\times64$ crops with dihedral augmentation from rows 416--2048 (800 non-overlapping patch equivalents). Sixteen-row gaps keep the three regions disjoint.

Pavia Centre~\cite{gamba2004pavia} was recorded by the ROSIS sensor over the centre of Pavia, Italy; the distributed scene has $1096\times715$ pixels at 1.3~m and 102 bands between 430 and 860~nm. For this dataset, \cite{chen2026twostage} uses the benchmark files released with PSRT~\cite{deng2023psrt}: 92 bands, a four-band multispectral image, four $256\times256$ test images and 142 validation and 1284 training patches of $64\times64$. These files are distributed through a file-sharing service that we could not access, so we rebuild the benchmark from the original scene to the same specification. We keep bands 11--102 (473--860~nm if the 102 bands are taken as evenly spaced over 430--860~nm), divide by the maximum of the retained bands (8{,}000~DN), apply the same blur and decimation as for Chikusei, and simulate the HR-MSI with the IKONOS responses. The test images are the four $256\times256$ tiles of the top-left $512\times512$ region. The 63 validation patches lie on a 32-pixel grid in rows 528--656, and training draws random $64\times64$ crops with dihedral augmentation from rows 672--1096 and from the strip to the right of the test region (rows 0--512, columns 528--715), 97 non-overlapping patch equivalents in total. Gaps of 16 pixels keep the regions disjoint. As a check on the rebuilt data, our GSA implementation reaches \pav{gsa-PSNR}~dB on our test images, against \pav{tipgsa-PSNR}~dB reported for GSA in~\cite{chen2026twostage}.

For both datasets we follow Wald's protocol~\cite{wald1997fusion}: the original image serves as ground truth and both observations are simulated from it, and every image, including every training crop, is degraded on its own with~\eqref{eq:obs}. Table~\ref{tab:protocol} summarizes the two protocols.

\textbf{Equal-budget benchmark.} Reported numbers come from different splits, normalizations and metric code (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}), so for the main comparison we retrain every learned method ourselves under one pipeline: SSR-NET~\cite{zhang2021ssrnet}, PSRT~\cite{deng2023psrt}, DCT~\cite{ma2024reciprocal}, MIMFormer~\cite{li2024mimformer} and DHIF-Net~\cite{huang2022dhif}, built from their authors' code\footnote{PSRT: github.com/Deng-shangqi/PSRT (commit 9c2be90); DCT: github.com/qingma2016/DCTransformer (bd75b15); MIMFormer: github.com/meiruni/MIMFormer (0904158); DHIF-Net: github.com/TaoHuang95/DHIF-Net (6434366). Only the numbers of input and output bands are changed; DHIF-Net's code covers $\times$8 and $\times$16, and at $\times$4 we use one of the two stride-4 layers of its $\times$16 operator.}. They cover a CNN, a pyramid window transformer, the dual-cross transformer that is among the strongest entries of~\cite{chen2026twostage}, a multiscale mixer, and a model-guided unfolding network whose blur, decimation and spectral operators are learned convolution layers. Every method, PUFormer included, is trained for the same 4~h on one Tesla T4, with the same random patches and augmentation, batch size, EMA weights and validation-only model selection. The published networks keep their official optimizer settings (Adam, L1 loss, learning rate $10^{-4}$, or $3\times10^{-4}$ for DHIF-Net, no weight decay or gradient clipping) and are trained in 32-bit precision as released; DCT and MIMFormer use gradient checkpointing~\cite{chen2016checkpoint} to fit into the 16~GB of a T4, which does not change what they compute. The test metrics are computed by the same code for all methods.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
We report the eight metrics of~\cite{chen2026twostage}, averaged over the test images: PSNR; SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim}; the spectral angle mapper (SAM, degrees, averaged over the pixels whose ground-truth and estimated spectra are both nonzero)~\cite{yuhas1992sam}; ERGAS with ratio 4~\cite{wald2000ergas}; Q2n~\cite{garzelli2009q2n} on $32\times32$ blocks, computed on the image in DN exactly as in the reference implementation of the pansharpening toolbox~\cite{vivone2015critical,deng2022machine}; the band-averaged correlation coefficient (CC); the spatial correlation coefficient (SCC)~\cite{zhou1998scc} of Sobel gradient magnitudes as in the same toolbox; and RMSE in DN. The headline PSNR is $10\log_{10}(1/\mathrm{MSE})$ over the whole normalized image, i.e., its peak is the dataset maximum. The PSNR values of~\cite{chen2026twostage} also use a fixed peak: for every row of their Chikusei table, $\mathrm{RMSE}\cdot10^{\mathrm{PSNR}/20}$ is between 16.6k and 17.1k~DN. Since our peak is lower (15{,}133~DN), our headline PSNR is conservative; with a 16.7k~DN peak it would be 0.86~dB higher. For Pavia Centre the same product lies between 7.7k and 8.4k~DN (median 8.07k), close to our peak of 8{,}000~DN, so there the PSNR values are on the same scale. The SSIM definition of~\cite{chen2026twostage} is not stated. We report SSIM as in the code released with PSRT~\cite{deng2023psrt}, whose benchmark data~\cite{chen2026twostage} adopts (Gaussian $11\times11$ window, $\sigma=1.5$, data range 1, i.e., the dataset peak), and additionally a stricter variant whose data range is each image's own maximum. We also report observation consistency, $\mathrm{PSNR}(\D\hat{\X},\YH)$ and $\mathrm{PSNR}(\R\hat{\X},\YM)$.

\subsection{Implementation}
PUFormer is implemented in PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch} and trained on Kaggle NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs (Table~\ref{tab:impl}). On Chikusei we train in two runs. The first run trains from scratch on one GPU with zero-padded operators and the L1 losses only. The final model fine-tunes it with the exact reflect-border operators and the full loss~\eqref{eq:loss} on two GPUs with distributed data parallelism~\cite{li2020ddp}. On Pavia Centre, a single run trains the model from scratch with the exact operators and the full loss on two GPUs, within one 12-hour session that also runs all tests. All runs use AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} ($\beta=(0.9,0.99)$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), a 1000-iteration linear warm-up followed by a cosine decay~\cite{loshchilov2017sgdr} to $10^{-3}$ of the peak rate that ends at the time budget, and gradient-norm clipping at 0.1. The weights with the best validation PSNR are tested once. The ablation and benchmark runs (Sections~\ref{sec:ablation} and~\ref{sec:bench}) train from scratch with these settings, the full loss and a single GPU for 4~h each; their models are evaluated without self-ensemble.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Ablation study}
\label{sec:ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} changes one module of the full model at a time and retrains each variant from scratch with the same budget (4~h on one T4, about 40{,}000 iterations for the full model). On Pavia Centre the full model and the zero-border variant were trained with three seeds; their PSNR varies by only \qone{abl-full-std} and \qone{abl-zeros-std}~dB across seeds, which sets the scale for the other differences. Chikusei repeats every variant, again with three seeds for these two rows.

\textbf{Data step and operator.} Removing the data-consistency step together with the residual input (row~(\qone{abl-nophys-row})) costs \qone{abl-nophys-absdelta}~dB and lowers the agreement of the reconstruction with the LR-HSI from \qone{abl-full-consH} to \qone{abl-nophys-consH}~dB. Keeping the exact kernel but replacing the reflect border by zero padding (row~(\qone{abl-zeros-row})) costs \qone{abl-seed-gain}~dB on average over the three seeds, and each zero-border seed is below each full-model seed. A blur kernel and spectral response learned with the network (row~(\qone{abl-learned-row})), the operator model of DHIF-Net and MHF-Net, cost \qone{abl-learned-absdelta}~dB. Both variants reproduce the LR-HSI about 3~dB worse than the exact model (\qone{abl-zeros-consH} and \qone{abl-learned-consH} against \qone{abl-full-consH}~dB). This is what Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch} predicts: their data steps move the ground truth, and the prior has to compensate. The compensation is tied to the operator used in training: Chikusei weights trained with a zero border fall from 58.60 to 49.52~dB validation PSNR when the exact border is substituted without retraining (Section~S-I of \supp). On the test images, the displacement of the ground truth by one LR-HSI data step, $\|\tilde{\D}^{\top}(\tilde{\D}-\D)\X\|$ relative to $\|\tilde{\D}^{\top}\tilde{\D}\X\|$, is \qone{fp-pavia-zeros-test}\% for the zero border and \qone{fp-pavia-learned-test}\% for the learned operator, and on $64\times64$ crops, where border pixels are a larger share, \qone{fp-pavia-zeros-crop}\% and \qone{fp-pavia-learned-crop}\%. Chikusei (rows~(\qone{kabl-nophys-row})--(\qone{kabl-nodeep-row})) shows the same ordering with larger gaps: without the data step the PSNR is \qone{kabl-nophys-absdelta}~dB lower, with a zero border \qone{kabl-zeros-absdelta}~dB and with a learned operator \qone{kabl-learned-absdelta}~dB, and the agreement with the LR-HSI drops from \qone{kabl-full-consH} to \qone{kabl-nophys-consH}, \qone{kabl-zeros-consH} and \qone{kabl-learned-consH}~dB.

The accuracy gap at the training blur is small; it becomes large when the blur changes (last column of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:operator}). Given the true blur at test time, the exact model loses at most \qone{abl-full-swaploss}~dB for $\sigma\in\{1.5,2.5,3.0\}$, the zero-border model with the same swap \qone{abl-zeros-swaploss}~dB, and the learned operator, which cannot be replaced, \qone{abl-learned-fixedloss}~dB. Without the swap the exact model loses \qone{abl-full-fixedloss}~dB: it is the most sensitive variant to a wrong operator, and the only one that fully recovers when given the right one. On Chikusei the difference is larger still: with the true blur the exact model loses at most \qone{kabl-full-swaploss}~dB, the zero-border model \qone{kabl-zeros-swaploss}~dB and the learned operator \qone{kabl-learned-fixedloss}~dB. A zero border thus prevents the swap from working, even though it differs from the exact operator only at the image border, because the prior has learned to compensate a border error tied to the training blur.

\textbf{Prior.} Cross-stage memory (row~(\qone{abl-nomem-row})), the residual input (row~(\qone{abl-nores-row})), the type of token mixer (rows~(\qone{abl-conv-row}) and~(\qone{abl-window-row}): a depth-wise convolution~\cite{chollet2017xception} or self-attention within $8\times8$ windows~\cite{liu2021swin} instead of transposed attention) and the number of stages (rows~(\qone{abl-k1-row})--(\qone{abl-k4-row})) change the PSNR on Pavia Centre by at most 0.06~dB, comparable to the spread across seeds. A one-stage model with \qone{abl-k1-params}~M parameters is within \qone{abl-k1-absdelta}~dB of the full model with \qone{abl-full-params}~M; part of this parity comes from the fixed time budget, in which the smaller model completes almost three times as many iterations. The SAM and SSIM loss terms (row~(\qone{abl-l1-row})) do not improve the PSNR, whereas on Pavia Centre deep supervision of the intermediate stages does (row~(\qone{abl-nodeep-row}); \qone{abl-nodeep-absdelta}~dB lower without it). On Chikusei, where the three seeds of the full model differ by only \qone{kabl-full-std}~dB, the same modules change the PSNR by between \qone{kabl-k2-delta} and \qone{kabl-conv-delta}~dB: memory \qone{kabl-nomem-delta}, residual input \qone{kabl-nores-delta}, window attention \qone{kabl-window-delta}, convolutional mixer \qone{kabl-conv-delta}, two and four stages \qone{kabl-k2-delta} and \qone{kabl-k4-delta}, SAM and SSIM loss terms \qone{kabl-l1-delta} and deep supervision \qone{kabl-nodeep-delta}~dB. Several of these simplifications are slightly better than the full model, so its prior is not optimal for this dataset, and deep supervision, which helps on Pavia Centre, does not help here. Only a single stage costs noticeably (\qone{kabl-k1-absdelta}~dB): on the larger, 128-band problem at least two stages are needed. We keep the full model for all other experiments, but the ablation shows that PUFormer's accuracy depends more on its exact data-consistency steps than on the particular design of its transformer prior.

\subsection{Where the error lies}
\label{sec:nullsplit}
Theorem~\ref{thm:kron}(c) splits the error of any fused image into a component in the observed subspace $\mathrm{row}(\D)+\mathrm{row}(\R)$, which contradicts the observations, and a component in the fusion null space, which no data step can correct and only a prior can reduce. Table~\ref{tab:null} applies this split to the test predictions of every method of the benchmark. Bicubic interpolation and GSA err mostly in the observed subspace: only \qone{null-chikusei-bicubic-share}\% and \qone{null-chikusei-gsa-share}\% of their squared error lies in the null space on Chikusei, so they do not even reproduce their inputs. The learned methods differ mainly in how much of the observed part they get wrong. PUFormer's error in the observed subspace is \qone{null-chikusei-obs-ratio} times smaller than MIMFormer's on Chikusei and \qone{null-pavia-obs-ratio} times on Pavia Centre, and \qone{null-chikusei-puformer-share}\% of its remaining error lies in the null space. The split also attributes PUFormer's margin: correcting MIMFormer's observed part alone would raise its PSNR by \qone{null-chikusei-consistency-gain}~dB on Chikusei, and the remaining \qone{null-chikusei-prior-gain}~dB come from a better estimate of the null-space component. The exact data steps account for the first part of the margin and the prior for the second; neither the attention mechanism nor any other single module of the prior is responsible for it (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Robustness of the long-trained models}
\label{sec:robust}
The long-trained models of Tables~\ref{tab:main} and~\ref{tab:pavia} behave like the ablation models under a blur mismatch (Table~S-II and Fig.~S3 of \supp). With the training operator, a blur with $\sigma$ between 1.5 and 3.0 costs 3.9--7.5~dB on Chikusei and \pav{blur-fixed-loss-min}--\pav{blur-fixed-loss-max}~dB on Pavia Centre; with the true blur swapped in, it costs at most 0.26 and \pav{blur-swap-loss-max}~dB. A spectral response shifted by 8~nm costs about 5~dB on Chikusei and is not compensated by swapping in the shifted response, because the priors rely on the training response beyond the data step; on Pavia Centre, whose IKONOS bands are 66--96~nm wide, the shift costs about 1~dB. Noise is the main weakness of models trained on noise-free data: at 30~dB SNR, PUFormer exceeds GSA by only 2.8~dB on Chikusei and by \pav{noise30-margin}~dB on Pavia Centre.

\subsection{One network for a family of observation models}
\label{sec:cond}
\label{sec:blind}
Table~\ref{tab:cond} tests the networks of Section~\ref{sec:randtrain} on conditions that leave the training protocol: blur widths from $0.5\sigma_0$ to $2\sigma_0$, spectral responses shifted by up to 12~nm, noise from 40 to 30~dB SNR, all three at once and, outside the operator family, anisotropic and defocus blurs and an HR-MSI shifted against the LR-HSI by a fraction of a pixel (every condition is listed in the supplementary material). The operators are estimated from each test pair with Theorem~\ref{thm:ident}. Within the family the estimate is exact: on every test image and condition, 30~dB SNR included, the blur width is recovered to within \qone{cond-chikusei-sigerr-max} and the spectral shift to within \qone{cond-chikusei-shifterr-max}~nm on Chikusei (\qone{cond-pavia-sigerr-max} and \qone{cond-pavia-shifterr-max}~nm on Pavia Centre).

Three results stand out. First, training over the family is what makes the network robust to noise and to combined changes. At 40--30~dB SNR the mean PSNR rises from \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-bench-puformer-fixed} to \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-cond-omc-estimated}~dB on Chikusei and from \qone{cond-pavia-noise-bench-puformer-fixed} to \qone{cond-pavia-noise-cond-omc-estimated}~dB on Pavia Centre, and under combined changes from \qone{cond-chikusei-combined-bench-puformer-estimated} to \qone{cond-chikusei-combined-cond-omc-estimated}~dB and from \qone{cond-pavia-combined-bench-puformer-estimated} to \qone{cond-pavia-combined-cond-omc-estimated}~dB. The price is a lower PSNR at the training operator, \qone{cond-chikusei-nominal-cond-omc-estimated} against \qone{chikusei4-psnr}~dB on Chikusei and \qone{cond-pavia-nominal-cond-omc-estimated} against \qone{pavia4-psnr}~dB on Pavia Centre.

Second, within the family, giving the data steps the estimated operator adds to randomized training alone. With observation-model conditioning the network is the most accurate in every group, for instance \qone{cond-chikusei-combined-cond-omc-estimated} against \qone{cond-chikusei-combined-cond-nocond-fixed}~dB for the same network trained on randomized data with the nominal operator under combined changes on Chikusei, and \qone{cond-pavia-combined-cond-omc-estimated} against \qone{cond-pavia-combined-cond-nocond-fixed}~dB on Pavia Centre. Observation-model conditioning adds little to operator conditioning alone (\qone{cond-chikusei-noise-cond-omc-estimated} against \qone{cond-chikusei-noise-cond-puformer-estimated}~dB under noise on Chikusei), and the exact operator stays ahead of the zero border (\qone{cond-chikusei-combined-cond-puformer-estimated} against \qone{cond-chikusei-combined-cond-zeros-estimated}~dB).

Third, outside the family the conditioned network is fragile. With an anisotropic or a defocus blur, the estimated isotropic operator is wrong; the conditioned network, which trusts its operator, falls to \qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-omc-estimated}~dB on Chikusei and \qone{cond-pavia-kernel-cond-omc-estimated}~dB on Pavia Centre, whereas the network trained on randomized data with the nominal operator keeps \qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-nocond-fixed} and \qone{cond-pavia-kernel-cond-nocond-fixed}~dB; given the true kernel, the conditioned network recovers (\qone{cond-chikusei-kernel-cond-omc-oracle} and \qone{cond-pavia-kernel-cond-omc-oracle}~dB). A sensitivity test shows why. With noise-free nominal data and a blur of $0.9\,\sigma_0$ in its data steps, a relative operator residual $\|(\tilde{\D}-\D)\X\|/\|\D\X\|$ of only \qone{sens-chikusei-res-0.9}\%, the conditioned network drops to \qone{sens-chikusei-cond-omc-0.9}~dB on Chikusei and the nominally trained one to \qone{sens-chikusei-bench-puformer-0.9}~dB, while the randomized-only network is unaffected (\qone{sens-chikusei-cond-nocond-0.9}~dB): as Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch} states, an exact data step trusts its operator. Exactness thus makes the network precise when the operator family contains the test conditions and fragile when it does not, so the family used for training and estimation has to cover the sensors it will meet. Finally, an HR-MSI misregistered by half a pixel costs every method more than 15~dB (\qone{cond-chikusei-misreg-cond-omc-fixed}~dB on Chikusei, with GSA at a similar loss): none of the methods compared here models registration errors.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\textbf{What the exact operators buy.} The exact operators matter less for accuracy at the training operator than for what they make possible. Their separable structure gives the data step in closed form and the null space of fusion explicitly, which turns error analysis into a split between what contradicts the observations and what only a prior can supply; PUFormer's error in the observed subspace is several times smaller than that of every retrained network. Because the operator enters only the data steps, it can be supplied, estimated from the test pair and replaced, and a single network covers a family of blurs, responses and noise levels. The same property makes the network depend on its operator: inside the family this is a strength, outside it a weakness, and the family used for training and estimation has to cover the sensors met in practice. The prior, in contrast, is a building block rather than a contribution: most of the margin over the retrained networks is already reached by the residual U-Net alone, and a convolutional mixer performs as well as transposed attention.

\textbf{Limitations.} All quantitative results with a reference rest on Wald's protocol, in which the observations are simulated from the reference, as in every benchmark of this field; in real data the border rule, the blur and the spectral response are only approximately known, and Theorems~\ref{thm:ident} and~\ref{thm:kron} assume that the operator family contains the true operator and that the blur is separable. Section~\ref{sec:cond} shows the consequence: outside the family, an operator-conditioned network that trusts a wrong operator loses several decibels, more than a network trained on randomized data without an operator, and a misregistration of half a pixel defeats every method compared here. Wider operator families, learned with the network or covered by the training distribution, and an explicit registration step are needed for real sensor pairs. The equal-budget benchmark gives every method 4~h on one GPU; the authors' own recipes do not improve the baselines under this budget, and an 8~h check narrows but keeps the gap, but fully converged comparisons were not run. The 17-method comparison of Tables~\ref{tab:main} and~\ref{tab:pavia} uses copied values on test images that follow the published protocol but are not the same pixels; our retrained baselines exceed their reported values by up to 3.6~dB, so these tables give context rather than a ranking, and the strongest reported methods could not be retrained because their code is not available. The test sets of Chikusei and Pavia Centre are small (8 and 4 images), so significance is assessed on the pooled settings.

\section{Conclusion}
We formulated hyperspectral and multispectral image fusion as maximum a posteriori estimation with parametric operators and unrolled it, and we proved three properties of the operators inside such a network: their parameters are identified by the observations, their separable structure gives the data step in closed form together with the null space of fusion, and an exact data step leaves the ground truth fixed while an inexact one moves it by a bounded amount. PUFormer, built on these results, is the most accurate of six networks retrained under one budget on Chikusei and Pavia Centre, its error lies almost entirely in the null space that no observation constrains, and trained over a family of observation models with estimated operators it keeps its accuracy under changed blurs, spectral responses and noise. Operator families that cover non-separable blurs and registration errors, and real sensor pairs, are the main directions for future work.
